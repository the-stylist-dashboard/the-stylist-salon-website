01 Why a trial matters more in wedding season

Wedding season mornings run on tight timelines, bright venue light, long hours, and tears. A look that holds in a salon chair under soft light can behave very differently at a venue at midnight. A trial lets you see the look in daylight and in photos, and lets the artist adjust the base, the eye intensity, and the lip shade before the day that counts.

A trial also catches skin reactions early. If a product does not suit your skin, you want to learn that at a trial, not on your wedding morning. Bring a note of any sensitivity or allergy, and say so before the trial starts.

Take trial photos in different light before you judge the look. Check it in daylight, in warm indoor light, and in a phone photo with flash, because wedding venues mix all three. A lip shade that looks perfect in the salon can read very differently under venue lights, and an eye look that photographs well may feel strong in person. Your notes from these checks are what turn a good trial into a wedding day plan you trust.

02 What a trial covers

A proper trial covers the full look: base, eyes, lips, hairstyle, and how they sit with your outfit and jewellery. Bring your outfit or a photo of it, your jewellery, and reference photos of looks you like and looks you do not want. The artist should ask about your venue, your ceremony time, and how long the look must last.

After the trial, check the look again after a few hours, not just in the mirror at the salon. Wear it, eat in it, step outside in it. Note what faded, what felt heavy, and what you kept touching up. That note is what the wedding day plan is built from.

03 When to book a trial around Diwali

Diwali falls on Sunday 8 November 2026, and the wedding weeks after it are the busiest of the year. Book your trial as soon as your wedding date is fixed, and leave at least a week between the trial and the wedding so adjustments can be made without rushing.

If you also want hair colour, a facial, or mehndi before the wedding, plan the order with the salon: colour first, facial three to five days before the function, mehndi timed so the colour deepens by the wedding day. One team planning the order beats separate bookings that clash.
