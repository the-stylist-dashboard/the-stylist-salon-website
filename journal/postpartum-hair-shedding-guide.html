Postpartum Hair Shedding: What's Normal and What Helps Cosmetically

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

Your baby is four months old, you are running on broken sleep, and now there are clumps of hair in the shower drain every morning. It is frightening the first time it happens. Many new mothers go through a phase of noticeably increased shedding a few months after delivery, and it usually settles on its own. Here is what is happening, what helps cosmetically, and when to check with a doctor.

01 Why this happens

During pregnancy, hormonal shifts keep more hairs than usual in their growing phase, which is why many women notice thicker, glossier hair while expecting. After delivery, as hormones settle, those extra hairs move into the resting and shedding phases around the same time. The result is a period of visibly increased shedding, often starting a few months after birth.

This is a timing shift, not damage. The hairs were always going to shed eventually; they are simply doing it together. For most women the shedding eases over the following months and the hair's usual fullness returns gradually. It can feel dramatic in the shower, but what you are seeing is catch-up shedding, not your hair falling out permanently.

02 When to check with a doctor

Most postpartum shedding follows the pattern above and resolves on its own. See a doctor or dermatologist if the shedding feels extreme to you, if you notice distinct bald patches or a widening part that keeps widening, or if shedding continues heavily well beyond the first year. Thyroid shifts and iron levels can change after delivery, and those are medical questions, not cosmetic ones. This guide covers only the cosmetic side; anything that worries you deserves a professional opinion.

03 Cosmetic care that helps hair look its best

While the cycle runs its course, gentle handling makes the regrowth phase look and feel better:

  • Be gentle when wet. Wet hair stretches and snaps more easily. Squeeze out water with a towel instead of rubbing, and detangle with a wide-tooth comb starting from the ends.
  • Loosen the styles. Tight ponytails and buns pull on already-vulnerable roots. A loose braid or low pony reduces the strain, and it keeps loose hairs contained instead of all over the house. Our night hair routine covers the gentlest ways to put hair up for sleep too.
  • Consider a trim. Shedding makes ends look thinner, and a tidy-up removes the wispy ends so the hair reads fuller. A fresh shape does more for confidence than any product at this stage.
  • Volumising styling helps. A blow-dry with lift at the roots, or even just flipping the part to the other side, makes new regrowth less noticeable while it fills in.

Be kind to yourself: this phase coincides with the hardest months of new motherhood. The hair almost always recovers. Spend your energy on rest and the baby, and let the routine stay simple.

04 What not to waste money on

Postpartum shedding attracts bold product claims. No shampoo, oil or serum can change a hormonal shedding cycle; they can only make the hair you have look better, which has value but is not a cure. Be especially sceptical of anything promising regrowth in weeks. Eat sensibly, rest when you can, and give it time. If a product claims to treat hair loss medically, that is a doctor's territory, not a cosmetic's.

Questions we hear at the salon

Is heavy shedding a few months after delivery normal?

Many new mothers notice visibly increased shedding starting a few months after birth. During pregnancy more hairs stay in the growing phase, and afterwards they shed together as hormones settle. It is a timing shift rather than damage, and for most women it eases over the following months. Anything that worries you deserves a doctor's opinion.

Will my hair grow back after postpartum shedding?

For most women, yes. The shedding phase runs its course and the hair's usual fullness returns gradually over the following months. Gentle handling, regular trims and volumising styling help it look its best meanwhile. If you see bald patches or shedding continues heavily beyond a year, see a dermatologist.

Can any shampoo or oil stop postpartum shedding?

No. The shedding follows a hormonal cycle, and no cosmetic product can change that timing. Shampoos, oils and serums can only make existing hair look and feel better, which is worthwhile but not a cure. Be sceptical of anything promising regrowth in weeks; that is medical territory, not cosmetic.

Should I cut my hair short while it is shedding?

You do not have to, but many new mothers find a tidy-up helps. Shedding makes ends look thinner, and removing the wispy ends makes hair read fuller. A fresh shape also lifts confidence during an exhausting phase. Choose whatever length is easiest to manage with a baby, not what anyone says you should do.
