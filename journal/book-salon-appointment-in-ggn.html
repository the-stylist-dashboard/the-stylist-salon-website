01 How booking works

Booking is by phone, and it takes about two minutes. Say the service, the day, and the time window that suits you, and your slot is held. You will be told how long the service takes, whether a patch test or trial is needed, and what the offer covers for your booking, before you visit.

Walk-ins are welcome, but a call holds your chair. For haircuts and cleanup, a walk-in usually works. For colour, keratin, bridal makeup, and full festive prep, a booked slot is the safer choice, because those services need a fixed block of time.

02 What to say on the call

Say your service first, then your hair or skin type in one line, then your function date. For example: a hair spa for dry, coloured hair, next Saturday, for a Diwali function on 8 November. That one sentence lets the team hold the right slot with the right stylist, instead of guessing.

Group bookings work well for wedding parties and Diwali prep, but they need an earlier call. If three or four people want services on the same day, say so when you book, so the salon can stagger start times instead of crowding everyone into one slot. A staggered plan means nobody waits in a chair for hours while someone else finishes, and the whole group still leaves ready together.

Mention recent colour, bleach, keratin, or smoothening work, and any skin sensitivity. Those details change the plan, the products, and sometimes the time needed, and it is better to sort them on the call than in the chair.

03 Diwali and wedding season slots

Diwali falls on Sunday 8 November 2026, and the week before it is the busiest week of the year. Colour should be booked three to five days before the festival, facials three to five days before, and mehndi timed so the colour deepens by the function. If your date is fixed, book now rather than in the last week.

Wedding season slots after Diwali go next. If you have a wedding date, say it on the call, because bridal trials and wedding day looks are booked as a pair, not as separate one-off slots.
