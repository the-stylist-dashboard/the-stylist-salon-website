This guide covers what a facial actually does, how to pick the right one, and when to book so the glow lands on time.

01 What a facial actually does

A facial is a deep, professional clean plus targeted treatment for your skin type. The steps are consistent: cleansing, gentle exfoliation to remove dead skin, steam or extraction to clear clogged pores, a massage to improve circulation, and a mask chosen for what your skin needs right now. The massage and the mask are the parts that give you the visible glow. What a facial does not do is change your skin permanently. It resets the surface, clears congestion, and hydrates. The glow lasts one to three weeks depending on your skin and your routine. Anyone promising a permanent transformation from one facial is selling, not treating.

02 Picking the right facial in Sector 43 for your skin

This is the part most people skip, and it matters the most. Oily or acne-prone skin needs a facial built for oil control: deep cleansing, light exfoliation, and masks that calm rather than coat. Dry skin needs hydration-focused facials with rich masks and gentle handling. Sensitive skin needs the mildest option on the menu, with a patch test if you react easily. Normal skin has the most choice. When you book a facial in Sector 43, tell the therapist your skin type honestly, including any products you use and any breakouts you have right now. A good therapist adjusts the facial to your face. A bad one runs the same routine on everyone.

03 Timing: when to book

Book your facial three to five days before any event, never the day before. Extraction and exfoliation can leave mild redness, and some skin reacts to new products with a day of sensitivity. Three to five days gives everything time to settle, and the glow peaks right around then anyway. If you have never had a facial at a particular salon, do not make your first visit the week of your wedding. Try it a month earlier so you know how your skin responds. For regular maintenance, once a month matches the skin's natural renewal cycle and keeps results steady.

04 What happens during the appointment

Plan for 60 to 90 minutes. You lie back while the therapist works through the steps: cleanse, exfoliate, steam, extraction where needed, massage, mask, and moisturise. Extraction can be mildly uncomfortable on clogged areas, but it should never be painful. Say so if it hurts; a good therapist adjusts pressure instead of pushing through. The mask stage is the quiet part, usually ten to fifteen minutes of just lying still. Bring no makeup to the appointment if you can. Clean skin at the start means better product absorption through the whole facial.

05 Aftercare: keep the glow

The first 24 hours after a facial matter. Do not touch your face more than necessary, skip heavy makeup, and avoid direct sun. Use a gentle moisturiser and sunscreen, and nothing with strong actives like retinoids or harsh acids for a day or two. Drink water. The glow fades fastest when you go straight back to heavy makeup and sun exposure. If your skin feels slightly tight or sensitive the next morning, that is normal and it settles. If you get real breakouts or a rash after a facial, call the salon and tell them what products were used, so it does not happen again.

The menu-name trap: facials with fancy names cost more but are not automatically better. Ask what products go on your face and what each step does. A simple, well-executed classic facial beats an expensive trendy one applied carelessly.

Which facial is best for oily or acne-prone skin?

Look for a facial built for oily skin: deep cleansing, gentle exfoliation, and oil-control masks. Avoid heavy cream-based facials, which clog oily skin. And be honest with your therapist about active breakouts, because extraction on inflamed acne makes it worse.

Can I get a facial right before my wedding or an event?

Not right before. Book your facial at least three to five days ahead so any redness or sensitivity settles. A facial the day before an event is how you end up hiding a reaction in photos. Plan ahead and your skin will thank you.

How often should I get a facial?

Once a month suits most skin types, because that matches the skin's natural renewal cycle. Very sensitive skin can go every six weeks. More often than monthly is unnecessary for most people and just spends your money faster.
