01 Planning makeup around your outfit

Bring a photo of your Diwali outfit, or wear a similar shade to your booking, because the makeup shades should sit well with the outfit, not fight it. A deep red or maroon outfit suits warm, rich shades, while a pastel outfit suits softer, lighter shades. Tell the salon about your jewellery too, because bold jewellery and bold makeup together can look heavy, while one bold element with the other kept soft usually photographs better.

Also share your skin type and any sensitivities, because the products and the base are chosen for your skin. Dry skin needs a moisturising base, oily skin needs a base that controls shine, and sensitive skin needs a gentler product list. A clear talk about your skin at booking avoids a base that cracks, slides, or feels heavy by mid-evening.

02 What makes Diwali makeup last

Long-lasting Diwali makeup starts with skin prep, not with extra layers of product. Clean, moisturised skin holds makeup far better than dry or oily skin, so a facial or a cleanup a few days before Diwali helps the makeup sit well. The base is then set properly, and the eye makeup is planned to survive heat and long hours without smudging.

Avoid trying a brand new look on the day of your main function. If you want a bold eye or a new shade, try it once before Diwali, so you know how it looks and how long it lasts on your skin. A trial also shows whether a shade suits you in daylight, not just under salon lights, and it gives time to change the plan if needed.

03 Timing your Diwali makeup booking

Book your Diwali makeup a few days ahead, in the week before Diwali on 8 November 2026, because makeup slots for the main functions fill fast. Tell the salon which function is your main one and what time it starts, so the booking can be placed with enough gap for dressing and travel. A rushed makeup slot, booked too close to the function start, is the most common reason for a look that feels unfinished.

If you have more than one function, plan the makeup for each one separately, because a day look and an evening look are different. A lighter look suits a daytime pooja or a family lunch, while a richer look suits the main evening function. Spacing the bookings across the week also keeps the salon visits calmer than one long, rushed day.
