Hair Colour and Allergies: The Patch Test Explainer

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

Nobody thinks about allergies until the itching starts. A patch test takes two minutes at the salon and 48 hours of waiting, and it is the cheapest insurance in all of hair colour. Here is what it is, how it is done properly, and what happens if you skip it.

01 What a patch test actually is

A small amount of the exact colour formula is dabbed behind your ear or on the inner elbow, then left alone for 48 hours. If the skin stays calm, the colour is considered safe to use on your head. If you get itching, redness, swelling or a rash at the test spot, that colour does not go near your scalp. Simple as that.

The test has to use the actual product being applied, not a similar one. A patch test with last year's formula tells you nothing about this year's tube. And the 48 hours matter. Some reactions show up on day one, others take the full two days.

02 Who must never skip it

  • First-time colour clients. You have no history to lean on, so the test is your history.
  • Anyone switching brands or shade families. A new formula is a new exposure.
  • Anyone returning after a long gap. Sensitivities can develop over time, even to products you once tolerated.
  • Anyone who has ever reacted. A previous reaction to any colour, henna or cosmetic deserves a fresh test and a frank conversation first.

Sensitive skin in general is also a flag. If your skin reacts to new skincare or jewellery easily, say so at the consultation. It does not disqualify you from colour, but it changes how carefully the colourist proceeds.

03 How a salon should do it

The test happens at least 48 hours before the appointment, using the mixed formula, and the result gets noted before colour day. What does not count as a patch test: "we have coloured you before with something similar," a quick dab done an hour before the service, or skipping it because you are in a hurry.

If a salon waves it off, that tells you something about the salon. At our chair, a first-time colour without a patch test does not happen, even if it means rescheduling. Doing box dye at home? The pack instructions include a patch test procedure. Follow it instead of trusting the brand name.

04 If your skin reacts

Rinse the product off with plenty of water and do not reapply it, not even a different shade from the same line. For anything beyond mild, brief irritation, see a doctor or dermatologist rather than trying to manage it yourself. Take the product name with you so the doctor knows what was involved.

A reaction does not always end your colour journey. Techniques that keep colour off the scalp, like foiled highlights, are sometimes an option, and gentler formulas exist. But that decision belongs in a consultation with full disclosure, not in a rushed appointment. If henna is on your mind as an alternative, read our henna vs hair dye guide first, because natural does not automatically mean safe either.

05 The myths that cause trouble

"Ammonia-free means safe." Ammonia-free only means one ingredient is absent. The colour still contains other active ingredients your skin can react to.

"Herbal or natural means safe." Henna, "herbal black" and plant-based colours can also trigger reactions, and black henna in particular has a rough reputation. Our treatment comparison covers a similar honesty principle: labels describe marketing, patch tests describe your skin.

The non-negotiable: 48 hours, the real formula, on your skin, before colour day. Everything else is commentary.

Questions we hear at the salon

What is a patch test for hair colour?

A patch test applies a small amount of the actual hair colour behind your ear or on the inner elbow, 48 hours before the appointment. If the skin stays calm, the colour is considered safe to use. Any itching, redness or swelling means the colour should not go on your head.

Do I need a patch test if I have coloured my hair before?

Yes, especially if you are switching brands, trying a new shade family, or returning after a long gap. Sensitivities can develop over time, so a clean history does not guarantee a clean future. Most salons repeat the test when the formula changes, and that caution is worth respecting.

What should I do if I react to hair dye?

Rinse the product off immediately with plenty of water and do not reapply it. For anything beyond mild, brief irritation, see a doctor or dermatologist rather than self-treating. Tell your colourist exactly what happened so safer alternatives like scalp-avoiding highlights can be discussed.

Are ammonia-free or natural hair colours allergy-safe?

Not necessarily. Ammonia-free only means one ingredient is absent, and natural or herbal colours, including henna and black henna, can also trigger reactions. No label exempts you from a patch test. The test checks your skin against the actual product, whatever the marketing says.
