01 Designs that suit Diwali

For Diwali, the designs that work best are paisley and floral patterns on the back of the hand, mandala circles on the palm, and jaal work that fills the hand without taking hours. Arabic style designs, with bold outlines and open spaces, suit people who want a striking look in less time.

If you cook, clean, or host during Diwali, a hand design that leaves your palms partly free is more practical than a dense full-arm design. Tell your mehndi artist how you will spend the next day, because that decides how much coverage makes sense.

02 Party mehndi versus bridal mehndi

Party mehndi is lighter and quicker, usually done on the hands in about thirty to sixty minutes, and it suits Diwali, sangeet, and wedding guest looks. Bridal mehndi is denser, covers hands and arms, and takes much longer, which is why brides book it as a separate appointment days before the wedding.

Children and guests at a Diwali gathering often want a small design too, and a quick motif on one hand suits them well. A small mandala, a simple floral trail, or a few paisleys take only minutes and still look festive. If you are hosting, book your own design first and add guest designs after, so your mehndi gets the time it needs before the gathering starts.

For Diwali as a guest or host, party mehndi is the right call. Save the full bridal style for a wedding, where the extra hours and coverage are part of the occasion.

03 Aftercare for a deeper colour

Let the mehndi dry fully before you touch it, and keep it on for as long as you can, ideally overnight. Once you scrape it off, avoid washing that hand for a few hours, and use a little oil on the design. Heat from cooking and repeated washing fade the colour faster, so plan kitchen work around your mehndi day where you can.

Colour varies by skin and body heat, so do not compare your stain with someone else's on the same day. A design applied a day or two before Diwali usually shows its deepest colour on the festival night itself, which is the point of the timing.
