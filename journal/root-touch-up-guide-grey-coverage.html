Root Touch-Ups: Timing, Coverage and the Line of Demarcation

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

Three weeks after a perfect colour, the mirror shows a dark stripe at the parting. At four weeks it is a headline. Root regrowth is the least glamorous part of hair colour, and it is the part that decides whether the whole thing looks expensive or neglected.

01 The line of demarcation, explained

The line of demarcation is the visible boundary where your natural regrowth meets the coloured lengths. It is not a flaw in the colourist's work. It is physics. The higher the contrast between your roots and your colour, the sharper that line looks. Light blonde on dark roots shows in two weeks. A brown close to your natural shade can hide for two months.

This is why the same colour looks high-maintenance on one person and effortless on another. It was never about the shade alone. It was always about the gap between the shade and what grows out of your head.

02 Timing it right

Hair grows roughly a centimetre a month, and that centimetre is the whole story. With visible greys, book every three to four weeks, because grey regrowth against coloured hair reads instantly. Without greys, the schedule loosens: six to eight weeks for a shade near your natural colour, four to six for something lighter.

A practical rule from the chair: when the regrowth reaches about two finger-widths at the parting, it is time. Stretch much beyond that and the touch-up starts behaving like a correction, which costs more and takes longer than the appointment you skipped.

03 Touch-up vs root smudge vs full recolour

These are three different services, and booking the wrong one wastes money. A root touch-up colours only the new growth, leaving the lengths alone. This is what most people need most of the time. A root smudge or shadow deliberately blurs the regrowth line with a softer, deeper tone at the roots, and it is usually paired with balayage or highlights to make grow-out graceful.

A full recolour is only needed when the lengths themselves have faded or shifted shade. The most common damage we see comes from the opposite mistake: permanent colour dragged through the lengths at every visit. Overlapped colour goes darker, duller and more brittle each time. Roots only, every time, unless the lengths genuinely need refreshing.

04 The grey percentage changes everything

Scattered greys at the temples behave differently from fifty percent grey across the crown. Light grey coverage can stretch a few extra weeks because the contrast is lower. Heavy grey coverage shows fast and shows hard, which is exactly why so many people feel trapped on the four-week cycle.

If that cycle is wearing you down, it is worth knowing there are middle paths. Grey blending weaves lighter pieces around the greys so the regrowth line softens instead of shouting. It does not eliminate maintenance, but it changes the rhythm of it.

05 Stretching the gap between visits

Root touch-up powders and tinted dry shampoos are genuinely useful for camouflaging the parting between appointments. A zigzag part or some lift at the crown breaks up the straight line of regrowth and buys you a week or two. Washing less often helps too, since every wash fades the colour sitting closest to the scalp.

The honest math: a touch-up every four weeks costs less per year than a correction every six months. Skipping appointments to save money usually achieves the opposite.

What you should not do is box-dye the roots yourself between salon visits. Home colour rarely matches the salon shade exactly, and mismatched roots are harder to fix than grown-out ones. Our colour fade guide covers how to keep the whole head looking fresh between touch-ups.

Questions we hear at the salon

How often should I get a root touch-up?

With visible greys, every three to four weeks keeps the regrowth line invisible. Without greys and with a shade close to your natural colour, six to eight weeks works. Light or fashion shades on dark roots show the line fastest and need the tightest schedule.

What is the line of demarcation in hair colour?

It is the visible boundary where your natural regrowth meets the coloured lengths. The higher the contrast between the two, the sharper the line looks. Root smudging and grey blending are techniques designed to soften or disguise it rather than eliminate it.

Can I stretch my root touch-up longer?

Yes, with a few tricks. Root touch-up powders and tinted dry shampoos camouflage the parting between visits. Wearing your hair with a zigzag part or added volume at the crown also hides regrowth. But stretching beyond eight to ten weeks usually means the correction costs more than the touch-up would have.
