01 Engagement look versus bridal look

Engagement makeup is usually softer than bridal makeup, with lighter base work and a focus on eyes or lips, not both at full strength. The aim is a look that reads well in photos but still looks like you when guests meet you up close.

Your outfit sets the direction. A pastel outfit suits soft tones, while a deeper outfit can carry a stronger lip or eye. Bring an outfit photo to the consultation so the makeup and hair are planned together, not in isolation.

02 What a trial covers

A trial tests the base shade in daylight and in evening light, checks how the look holds after hours, and confirms that nothing irritates your skin. It also settles the hairstyle, so hair and makeup do not compete on the day.

If your engagement is close to your wedding, say so at booking. The team can plan the engagement look as a step toward the bridal look, or keep them clearly different, depending on what you want.

03 Skin prep before the engagement

Book a facial or cleanup at least a week before the engagement, not the day before. Skin needs a few days to settle, and a last minute facial can leave redness that makeup then has to cover.

Keep your home routine simple in the final week: cleanse, moisturise, and avoid trying a new product for the first time. New products close to a function are the most common cause of a surprise reaction.

04 On the day: timing and touch-ups

Arrive with clean, moisturised skin and your outfit plan ready. Hair usually comes first or alongside base work, depending on the hairstyle, and the team will set the order when you book.

Carry your lip shade and blotting paper for touch-ups through the evening. A small touch-up kit keeps the look fresh for late photos without redoing the full makeup.

05 Hair to match engagement makeup

Hair and makeup should be planned together, because a heavy hairstyle with strong makeup can look too much, while soft waves with a soft face can look unfinished. The trial is the place to test the pair, not the engagement evening.

Open hair suits many engagement outfits, but it needs a style that holds through hours of greetings and dancing. Half tied styles and soft curls hold better than a loose blowout on a long evening.

Bring any hair accessory or pins you plan to wear to the trial. An accessory changes the balance of the whole look, and testing it early avoids a last minute change that throws off the makeup plan.

For an evening engagement, check the trial look under warm indoor light as well as daylight. Makeup that looks perfect by a window can read differently under venue lights, and a quick check in both lights avoids a surprise on the night.

Questions people ask

How is engagement makeup different from bridal makeup?

Engagement makeup is usually softer, with lighter base work and a focus on eyes or lips, while bridal makeup is fuller and built to last a longer ceremony day.

Do I need a trial for engagement makeup?

A trial helps if you have sensitive skin, a new outfit colour, or a look you have not worn before. It tests shade, wear, and hairstyle together.
