Why Hair Colour Fades (and How to Slow It Down)

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

The copper you left the salon with was glorious. Three weeks later it is a vague warm brown, and you are wondering whether the colourist did something wrong. Probably not. Colour fade is part chemistry and part habit, and in Gurugram the water alone does half the damage.

01 The usual suspects

Every wash lifts a little colour out of the hair. Hot water lifts more, because heat opens the hair's outer layer and lets pigment escape faster. Harsh shampoos, especially strong clarifying and anti-dandruff formulas, strip colour aggressively. Sunlight bleaches the top layers over time, which is why the crown fades first. And some shades are simply born to fade: reds and coppers lose intensity fastest, while deep browns and blacks hold on longest.

Porous hair fades faster too. If your hair has been lightened, heat-styled daily or chemically treated before, it drinks colour in and then lets it slip out just as quickly. The rougher the hair's surface, the shorter the colour's life.

02 Gurugram's specific problem: the water

Gurugram runs on mineral-heavy groundwater, and those minerals do not rinse clean. They build up on the hair, dulling the tone and gradually shifting cool shades toward brassy. Clients who move here from softer-water cities notice it within a month: the same colour, the same shampoo, a flatter result.

A shower filter helps reduce the mineral load, though it will not fix it entirely. An occasional chelating wash lifts the buildup off, but use it sparingly because it takes some colour with it. 03 The wash routine that protects colour

  • Wash in lukewarm water. Hot showers feel good and fade colour fast. Lukewarm cleans just as well.
  • Use a sulphate-free shampoo. Sulphates are efficient cleansers and efficient colour strippers. A colour-safe formula is the single easiest upgrade.
  • Wash less often. Two to three washes a week with dry shampoo between beats a daily wash for colour life.
  • Condition properly. Colour-processed hair needs the moisture barrier that conditioner rebuilds. Skipping it speeds up fade.

For reds and fashion shades, a colour-depositing conditioner or mask in your shade is worth its price. It tops up pigment with every wash and can double the time between salon refreshes.

04 Between-appointment maintenance

A gloss or toner refresh every six to eight weeks is the professional answer to fade. It is a short appointment, about 45 minutes, that restores tone and shine without reprocessing the hair. Think of it as servicing the colour rather than redoing it.

Heat protection before styling matters more on coloured hair than on virgin hair, since heat accelerates the fade you are fighting. And regular trims keep the porous, fade-prone ends from dragging the whole head down. A monthly hair spa also helps, because conditioned hair simply holds colour better than dry hair.

05 What not to do

Do not reach for a clarifying shampoo the week after colouring. Do not wash daily with a strong anti-dandruff formula and expect the shade to survive. Do not assume oiling protects colour either; oil does not seal pigment in, and heavy oiling followed by harsh shampooing to remove it is one of the faster ways to strip a fresh shade.

The 48-hour rule: give fresh colour two days before the first wash. The shade settles, the hair's outer layer calms down, and you start the fade clock later.

Questions we hear at the salon

How long should hair colour last before fading?

Permanent colour on the lengths holds for six to eight weeks before visibly dulling, though roots show sooner. Semi-permanent and fashion shades fade much faster, often within six to twelve washes. Reds and coppers lose intensity quickest because their pigment molecules are the largest and wash out first.

Does hard water really fade hair colour?

Yes. Gurugram's mineral-heavy water leaves deposits on the hair that dull the tone and can shift cool shades brassy. A chelating or clarifying wash once in a while removes buildup, though use it sparingly since it also strips some colour. A shower filter helps reduce deposits but does not eliminate them.

Should I wash coloured hair less often?

Fewer washes mean less fading, since every wash lifts some colour. Two to three washes a week with dry shampoo in between works for most people. If your scalp gets oily fast, a sulphate-free shampoo used properly cleans well without being as harsh as daily strong shampoos.

What is a gloss or toner refresh?

A gloss or toner refresh is a short salon service that revives the tone of faded colour without reprocessing the hair. It takes about 45 minutes and restores shine and depth. Most colourists suggest one every six to eight weeks to keep the shade looking freshly done between full colours.
