01 Simple mehndi designs that suit most functions

A simple mehndi design is the right pick for most family functions, because it looks pretty, takes less time, and still photographs well. Arabic patterns with bold lines and open spaces are popular for guests, because they dry faster and leave a clean, dark stain. A simple design on the palms and the back of the hands is enough for most guests, and it leaves the fingers free for the rest of the evening.

If you are short on time, ask for a design that covers the palms only, because it is quicker to apply and quicker to dry. A small mandala on the palm, with a few leaves and dots around it, is a classic choice that never looks wrong. Tell the artist how much time you have at booking, so the design can be planned to fit, instead of being rushed at the end.

02 Bridal mehndi designs for the wedding day

Bridal mehndi is a full design that covers the hands up to the elbows and the feet up to the ankles, with fine lines, paisleys, peacocks, and small portraits of the couple hidden in the pattern. It takes three to four hours, so the bride should plan her day around it, with food, water, and rest breaks built in. The design is planned with the bride before the day, so the pattern, the density, and the figures are all agreed in advance.

A good bridal design keeps the lines fine and the spacing even, because that is what gives a deep, even colour. The bride should sit still while the mehndi dries, because smudging ruins the fine work, and a smudged design cannot be fixed once the paste is on. Ask the artist to show you a sample of their bridal work before you book, so you know the quality you are getting.

03 How to pick a design for your outfit

Most mehndi outfits are green, yellow, or orange, so the design should be picked to suit the outfit and the jewellery, not just the hands. A dense bridal design suits a heavy outfit, because the hands and the outfit balance each other, while a simple design suits a lighter outfit, because the hands stay the focus. Tell the artist your outfit colour at booking, so the design can be planned to match instead of clashing with it.

If your outfit has a lot of work on the sleeves, keep the mehndi on the arms a little simpler, because a heavy design under heavy sleeves is hidden anyway. A design that shows on the hands and the feet is what photographs best, so spend the time there. Bring a photo of your outfit to the salon if you can, so the artist can plan the design around the real colours and the real fabric.

Questions people ask

How long does mehndi take to apply?

A simple design takes 30 to 45 minutes, while a full bridal design takes three to four hours. Book early in the day so the mehndi has time to dry and develop colour.

Which mehndi design suits a first-timer?

A simple Arabic design suits a first-timer, because it is quick, looks clean, and is easy to wear. A mandala on the palm with leaves around it is a safe and pretty choice.

How do I make mehndi colour darker?

Keep the paste on for at least six hours, avoid water for the first few hours after removal, and keep your hands warm. A deeper colour comes from longer contact, not from extra paste.
