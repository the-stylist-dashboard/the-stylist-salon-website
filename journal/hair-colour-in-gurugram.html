01 Global colour, highlights, or balayage

Global colour covers the full head in one shade and suits clients who want even coverage, including grey coverage. Highlights add lighter strands through the hair for depth and movement, while balayage paints lighter pieces by hand for a softer grow-out.

Your choice should follow your upkeep habit. Global colour needs root touch-ups on a steady cycle, while balayage grows out more gently. Say how often you will visit before you pick, and let the stylist match the technique to that answer.

02 Why a patch test matters

A patch test checks how your skin reacts to colour before a full application. It takes a few minutes to apply and a short wait, and it protects you from a reaction on the day of a function.

Tell the stylist about past colour, keratin, or smoothening work, and about any sensitivity you have had. Colour over old chemical work behaves differently, and a stylist who knows the history can adjust the formula and timing.

03 Aftercare that keeps colour fresh

Wash with lukewarm water, use products made for coloured hair, and give the colour a day or two before the first wash after the visit. Heat styling every day fades colour faster, so use a heat protectant when you do style.

Book colour three to four days before a function, not on the morning of it. That gap lets the shade settle, lets you check it in daylight, and leaves time for a small fix if one is needed.

04 Colour for Diwali week

Diwali on 8 November 2026 is a common reason to colour, and slots in that week go early. Book the consultation first, then the colour slot, so the patch test and the application both fit before your function.

Pair colour with a hair spa a few days later if your hair feels dry after colouring. A hair spa adds moisture and shine without changing the shade, and it helps the colour look its best in photos.

05 How often colour needs a touch-up

Global colour usually needs a root touch-up every four to six weeks, depending on how fast your hair grows and how much grey you have. Highlights and balayage stretch longer between visits, because the grow-out is softer and less visible.

Grey coverage needs the most regular cycle, because new growth shows against a darker shade. If you want less frequent visits, ask about a shade closer to your natural colour, which hides growth better.

Between visits, protect the colour with lukewarm washes and products made for coloured hair, and book a hair spa if the hair feels dry. A well kept colour lasts longer and looks better right up to the next appointment.

If you swim or spend long hours in strong sun, say so at the consultation, because both fade colour faster. A stylist who knows your routine can suggest a shade and a care plan that holds up to real life, not just to the salon chair.
