01 What balayage is, and how it is done

Balayage is a freehand colour technique, where the stylist paints lighter shades onto the mid-lengths and ends of the hair, leaving the roots darker, so the colour melts from dark to light. The stylist picks the shades for your skin tone and your natural hair colour, then paints them on in soft strokes, so no two heads of balayage look the same. The visit takes a few hours, because the colour needs time to lift and the toner needs time to settle.

A patch test comes first if you have never had colour before, and the stylist will check your hair history, because balayage is a lightening service and it is not right for hair that is badly damaged. Tell your stylist how light you want to go, because a subtle caramel suits a first-timer, while a bolder blonde suits someone who wants a clear change. A good stylist will show you photos of finished hair, so you can pick with confidence.

02 Balayage and highlights, what is different

Balayage gives a softer, more natural finish than foil highlights, because the colour is painted on by hand and blends into the hair, while highlights give a more even, striped finish from root to tip. Balayage also grows out far more gracefully, because there is no hard line at the roots, so you can wait months between visits. Highlights need a touch-up sooner, because the regrowth shows as a clear line.

03 Looking after balayage at home

After balayage, wash with a mild, sulphate-free shampoo and lukewarm water, and use a colour-safe conditioner, because harsh shampoo fades the colour fast. A purple shampoo once a week keeps blonde and caramel tones from turning brassy, but use it sparingly, because too much can dull the colour. Keep the hair away from chlorine and hard water where you can, and use heat protection before styling.

A hair spa every few weeks keeps balayage hair soft, because lightening can dry the hair out over time. If the ends start to feel rough or the colour looks dull, come back for a check, because a toner refresh or a trim is often all it needs. Book a toner refresh every six to eight weeks, so the colour stays fresh without a full redo.
