Henna vs Hair Dye: The Trade-Offs Nobody Puts on the Box

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

Every month someone sits in my chair and says some version of the same sentence: I have been using henna for years, and now I want highlights. Then I have to explain why those two things are on a collision course, and why nobody told her that when she bought the first box.

01 What henna does well

Pure henna covers grey with a reddish-brown or copper tone, costs very little, and many people find it leaves the hair feeling thicker and conditioned. It needs no developer, no salon visit and no patch-test drama in most cases. For someone who wants simple grey coverage in a warm tone and never plans to lighten, it is a perfectly reasonable choice.

The catch is in the word pure. What the box promises and what is inside it are not always the same thing, which brings us to the section most henna users have never read.

02 The permanence trap

Henna does not wash out. It binds to the hair and stays there until the hair grows out or is cut off. That permanence is fine until you want something else. Lightener applied over henna can behave unpredictably, sometimes lifting unevenly, sometimes refusing to lift at all, sometimes producing tones nobody asked for.

This is why every colourist asks about henna before mixing anything. A strand test is the only honest way to find out what your henna-treated hair will do under lightener. Growing henna out takes years on long hair, so the decision you make today limits your options for a long time. If there is even a small chance you will want highlights, balayage or any lightening in the next couple of years, henna deserves a second thought.

03 The black henna problem

So-called black henna or herbal black is rarely pure henna. These products typically contain added chemicals and metallic salts to achieve the dark result quickly. They carry a higher risk of scalp reactions than pure henna, and they are the worst offenders when it comes to reacting with salon lightener.

If you have used black henna, tell your colourist before any chemical service, full stop. Do not assume it has "washed out" after a few months. It has not. Our patch test guide covers why natural-looking labels still deserve caution.

04 What hair dye does well, honestly

Hair dye offers precise shade control, reliable grey coverage in any tone from black to blonde, and the ability to lighten. Want chocolate brown this year and caramel highlights next year? Dye can do both, in sequence, predictably. That flexibility is what you are paying for.

The downsides are real too. There is a regrowth line to maintain, the upkeep costs more than a henna box, shades fade and need refreshing, and repeated lightening weakens the hair over time. Dye also demands a patch test before first use. Neither option is free of trade-offs. They just charge you in different currencies.

The two-year rule: if there is any chance you will want highlights, balayage or lighter colour in the next two years, think twice before henna. Reversing that decision is the hardest correction we do.

Questions we hear at the salon

Can I get highlights after using henna?

Sometimes, but only after a strand test. Lightener over henna can behave unpredictably, and the result depends on the henna type and how long ago it was applied. Pure henna from over a year ago is usually workable. Recent applications or black henna often rule lightening out entirely.

Does henna cover grey hair well?

Pure henna covers grey with a reddish-brown or copper tone, not a natural brown or black. The colour also varies with your base shade and processing time. Many people follow henna with indigo for a darker result, but that two-step process adds time and its own permanence.

Is black henna safe for hair?

So-called black henna is rarely pure henna and often contains added chemicals and metallic salts. It carries a higher risk of scalp reactions and reacts badly with salon lightener. If you have used black henna, tell your colourist before any chemical service and expect a strand test first.

Which is better for hair health: henna or dye?

Pure henna coats the hair and many people find it conditioning, while repeated dyeing, especially lightening, weakens the hair over time. But henna's permanence is its own risk: once applied, your future colour options narrow sharply. Health is not just about today's application but about what it locks you out of tomorrow.
