Global Hair Colour: Choosing a Shade for Indian Skin Tones

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

A client walked in last month with a photo of a chocolate-brown global colour and one firm condition: it had to look like her hair, only better. Not lighter, not trendier. That is the entire brief for a good global colour on Indian skin. One even shade, roots to ends, that looks like it could have grown out of your head.

01 What global colour actually means

Global colour is one shade applied across the whole head, roots to ends. No highlights, no lowlights, no dimension games. It is the most committed colour service there is, because every strand is processed and every millimetre of regrowth shows. That regrowth line, the line of demarcation, is the price of admission.

People choose it for full grey coverage, for a richer version of their natural colour, or for a clean all-over change like black to brown. If you want movement and softness instead, placement techniques like balayage on Indian hair may suit you better. Global is for people who want one decisive shade.

02 The undertone rule

Most Indian skin carries warm or golden undertones. Shades with warmth in them, chocolate, mahogany, warm espresso, sit on warm skin like they belong there. Cool, ashy shades can work too, but they need more maintenance and they can make warm skin look flat if the tone is off.

A quick at-home check: hold gold and silver jewellery against your face in daylight. If gold looks harmonious and silver looks stark, your undertone leans warm and warm shades will flatter you most. It is a rough guide, not a law, but it beats picking a shade from a box photo under tube light.

Two honest notes from the chair. First, flat blue-black on warm skin often reads wig-like; a soft black with brown depth looks far more natural. Second, burgundy and wine shades look electric for the first two weeks and then settle into something subtler. If you love the day-one intensity, budget for gloss refreshes.

04 What goes wrong on dark hair

Lifting black hair to a light global brown means passing through an orange stage. There is no way around it, only through it, which is why dramatic lightening sometimes needs two sittings. Box dye on dark hair barely moves the needle; the developer in home kits is not built for a three-level lift, so people end up with patchy, brassy results and then come in for correction.

Banding is the other classic problem. When colour is overlapped onto already-coloured lengths at every touch-up, the mid-lengths go darker and duller than the roots, and you get visible bands. A good colourist applies permanent colour to roots only and refreshes the lengths with a gentler gloss. If your history includes henna, read our henna vs hair dye guide before booking anything.

05 Keeping the shade alive

Global colour fades fastest where the sun and water hit it hardest. In Gurugram that means hard water dulling the tone and strong sun bleaching the top layers. Wash in lukewarm water with a sulphate-free shampoo, wash less often than you think you need to, and get a gloss or toner refresh between full colours.

The shade test: if you cannot decide between two browns, pick the darker one. Colour always looks a touch lighter in salon lighting than in daylight, and going darker later is easier than lifting back up.

Root touch-ups every three to four weeks if you have greys, longer if you do not. And be straight with your colourist about your budget for upkeep before you pick the shade. A colour you cannot maintain is a colour you will resent.

Questions we hear at the salon

Will a global colour suit my wheatish skin?

Yes, if the shade respects your undertone. Warm browns like dark chocolate, mahogany and warm espresso flatter wheatish skin beautifully. The shades that clash are usually icy ash tones or flat blue-black, which can make warm skin look dull instead of glowing.

Can I go from black hair to brown in one sitting?

Going one to two levels lighter is realistic in a single sitting on virgin hair. Jumping from black to a light brown needs more lift, and the hair passes through a warm orange stage first. Your colourist may suggest two sittings a few weeks apart to reach a lighter target without over-processing.

How often do roots need a touch-up after global colour?

With grey hair, expect a root touch-up every three to four weeks, since the contrast shows quickly. Without greys and with a shade close to your natural colour, you can stretch to six to eight weeks. Lighter shades on dark roots show the line fastest.

Does global colour damage hair more than highlights?

Global colour touches every strand, so the cumulative processing is higher than highlights, which leave sections untouched. The damage stays manageable with a good colourist and proper aftercare. Repeated overlapping of colour on already-coloured lengths causes most breakage, so roots-only application matters.
