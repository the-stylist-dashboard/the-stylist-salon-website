01 What nanoplastia is, and who it suits

Nanoplastia is a deep smoothing treatment that works on the hair fibre itself, so the result is not a coating that washes off in a week. The stylist washes the hair, applies the treatment, then seals it in with a flat iron, and the hair comes out softer, shinier, and far easier to comb. It suits frizzy hair, wavy hair that will not sit neatly, and hair that has gone dry from colour or heat styling.

It also suits people who want smooth hair but do not want the flat, pin straight look of rebonding, because nanoplastia keeps some natural movement in the hair. If your hair is very curly and you want it fully straight, straightening may suit you better, so say what finish you want at the consultation. A good stylist will tell you honestly which service matches the look you have in mind.

02 Nanoplastia and keratin, what is different

Nanoplastia and keratin both smooth the hair, but nanoplastia usually lasts longer and leaves the hair softer, because it works deeper in the hair fibre. Keratin is lighter and suits hair that only needs frizz control and shine, while nanoplastia suits hair that is rougher, drier, or harder to manage. Both need the same kind of aftercare, with mild shampoo and care around water for the first few days.

03 Looking after nanoplastia at home

After nanoplastia, wash with a mild, sulphate-free shampoo and lukewarm water, and avoid tying the hair tightly for the first two or three days. Do not wash the hair for the first 48 hours if your stylist advises it, because the treatment needs time to settle fully. A hair spa every few weeks keeps the softness going, and it helps the treatment last longer.

Avoid heavy oiling right after the treatment, because oil can weigh the hair down and dull the shine. If the hair starts to feel dry at the ends, a small trim and a spa are usually enough to bring it back. Book a review visit if anything feels wrong, because a quick check is better than waiting until the hair is hard to manage again.
