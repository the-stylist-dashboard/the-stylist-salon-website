Rebonding on Indian Hair: What to Expect

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

It is 7 AM, you have a 9 AM meeting in Cyber City, and your curly hair has decided today is the day it will not cooperate. You have spent years straightening it every other morning, and you are done. Rebonding promises to end that routine for good. Before you book the six-hour appointment, here is what actually happens, and what nobody tells you about the months after.

01 How Indian hair responds to rebonding

Indian hair is typically thick and dense, which is actually good news for rebonding. The treatment takes well to coarse hair and the results last. The trade-off is time: where fine hair might take three hours, thick Indian hair often needs five to six hours in the chair. Density also means more product and more careful sectioning, so pricing varies with hair length and density.

One thing to know upfront: rebonding removes volume along with the curl. If you love your hair's thickness and body, think hard before committing. The sleek look comes at the cost of that fullness.

02 The appointment, step by step

  1. Consultation and hair analysis. A good stylist checks your hair's history, bleach, colour, previous straightening, before committing to anything.
  2. Deep cleansing wash, then the rebonding cream is applied section by section and left to process.
  3. Rinse and straightening. Hair is rinsed, blow-dried straight, then a flat iron passes over small sections. This is the longest part of the day.
  4. Neutralizer. Applied to lock the new straight structure in, left to sit, then rinsed out.
  5. Final wash, conditioning mask, and a smooth blow-dry.

You will be in the chair most of the day. Eat beforehand and bring something to read.

03 The non-negotiable aftercare

The first 72 hours are sacred. No washing, no tying, no clips, no tucking behind your ears, no rain. Sleep with your hair loose and flat. Any bend or kink during this window can set permanently into the hair.

After that, sulphate-free shampoo, lukewarm water, and regular conditioning become your routine. Heat protectant before any hot tools. Gurugram's hard water is harsh on chemically treated hair, so consider a shower filter. Our hard water guide explains why it matters.

04 The regrowth reality

Rebonding is permanent on the hair it touches, which means it never washes out, it grows out. After three to four months you will see your natural texture returning at the roots while the lengths stay straight. That line where the two meet is the regrowth line, and it is the part people underestimate.

Most people get the new growth touched up every six to nine months. The touch-up only goes on the virgin roots, never over already-rebonded lengths, because reprocessing the same hair causes breakage. Budget for this rhythm before you start. It is a commitment, not a one-time expense.

05 Honest downsides

Rebonding is the most committing straightening treatment there is. The hair is chemically altered and there is no reversing it, you grow it out or cut it off. Over-processed hair can become dry and brittle, especially at the ends, and fine hair can look noticeably thinner. Swimming, especially in chlorinated pools, is rough on rebonded hair.

The 72-hour rule: set a phone reminder for exactly when your 72 hours end. Most aftercare slip-ups happen because people lose track of time, not because they do not care.

Questions we hear at the salon

How long does rebonding last on Indian hair?

The straightening is permanent on treated hair, so it lasts until it grows out, typically looking good for 6 to 9 months before the regrowth needs a touch-up. How it looks over time depends on aftercare. Harsh shampoos, frequent heat, and hard water wear the smoothness down faster.

Can rebonded hair be coloured?

Yes, but wait at least two to three weeks after rebonding and always do a strand test first. Rebonded hair has been through a strong chemical process, so adding colour too soon can cause dryness or breakage. Stick to gentler colour options and tell your colourist about the rebonding.

Is rebonding safe for thin hair?

It can be done on fine hair, but the result often looks flatter and thinner because rebonding removes volume. If your hair is fine, discuss this honestly with your stylist. Smoothening or nanoplastia may give you the sleek look with less volume loss, so weigh the trade-off before committing.

What happens if I tie my hair during the first 72 hours?

A ponytail holder or clip can leave a permanent kink in the setting hair. If it happens, tell your stylist immediately, since it can sometimes be corrected with heat early on. Prevention is far easier, so keep hair completely loose and untouched for the full 72 hours.
