Facials for Men: A First-Timer's Guide

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

Your sister has had forty facials. You have had zero, and the idea of lying in a chair while someone paints things on your face feels vaguely ridiculous. Then you catch yourself in a lift mirror after a week of late nights and realise your skin looks tired in a way soap is not fixing. Here is the thing nobody told you: a facial is mostly deep cleaning with better tools. Nothing mystical. Just an hour that your face actually needed.

01 Why men put this off, and why the skin does not care

Sun, sweat, shaving, and Gurugram dust hit men's skin hard, and most men answer with face wash and hope. That works until it does not. Clogged pores, rough texture, and that dull film that makes you look older than you are do not wash off with a bar of soap.

A facial is not vanity. It is maintenance, the same logic as servicing a bike. You can skip it for years and nothing dramatic happens, but the machine runs better when someone who knows what they are doing opens it up once in a while.

02 What actually happens, step by step

Knowing the sequence takes the awkwardness out. A standard facial runs about sixty minutes:

  • Consultation and cleanse: the therapist looks at your skin, asks about sensitivity, and removes the day's grime with a proper cleanser.
  • Exfoliation: dead skin is lifted with a scrub or a mild exfoliating product. This is the step that makes your face feel instantly smoother.
  • Steam: warm steam opens the pores so the next steps work deeper. It feels good. That is not a trick.
  • Extractions: clogged pores and blackheads are cleared manually. Slightly uncomfortable, over in minutes, and the reason your nose looks cleaner afterward.
  • Massage: cream or serum worked in with facial massage strokes. This is the part people fall asleep during.
  • Mask: a mask chosen for your skin type sits for ten to fifteen minutes while you do nothing. Bliss, once you stop checking your phone.
  • Moisturise and protect: moisturiser, and sunscreen if it is daytime. Done.

Nothing in that list requires courage. The strangest part is how normal it feels by minute twenty.

03 Before your appointment

Arrive with a clean-ish face and shave the evening before, not the morning of. Freshly shaved skin plus exfoliation equals stinging you did not need. If you have a beard, keep it clean and mention where you want products applied; the facial works around it fine.

Tell the therapist about sensitive skin, allergies, or anything you are using on your face. This is not small talk. It decides which products touch your skin for the next hour.

04 After: the first 48 hours

Mild pinkness for a few hours is normal. Do not scrub your face, do not try new products, and stay out of harsh sun the next day. Sunscreen is non-negotiable after a facial; your skin has been exfoliated and is more vulnerable than usual.

This is also why timing matters. Book three to four days before a wedding or event, never the morning of. Skin occasionally needs a day to settle, and you want that day to be a Tuesday, not your cousin's sangeet.

05 Picking the right facial

For a first-timer, a classic clean-up or basic facial is the right call. It covers deep cleansing, exfoliation, and a mask without overwhelming your skin. Fancier options with specialised serums or brightening treatments make more sense once you know how your skin reacts.

06 What a facial will not fix

One facial will not erase years of sun damage, and no facial treats medical skin conditions. What it does is reset the surface: cleaner pores, smoother texture, better hydration, and skin that takes in your regular products more effectively afterward. Think of it as the deep clean that makes your daily routine work better, not a replacement for one.

If your skin has persistent issues that never improve with regular care, a dermatologist is the right next stop, not a stronger facial. A good salon will tell you that.

First-timer rule: Do not book a facial the week you start a new face wash, a new sunscreen, or any active product. If your skin reacts, nobody will know which of the three caused it. Change one variable at a time.

Questions we hear at the salon

Is a facial different for men than for women?

The steps are the same but the emphasis shifts. Men's skin tends to be oilier with larger pores and daily shaving irritation, so the therapist spends more time on deep cleansing, exfoliation and calming the shaved areas. The products chosen are usually lighter and fragrance-free. A good salon adjusts the facial to the face in front of them either way.

Should I shave before my facial?

Shave the evening before, not the morning of. Freshly shaved skin is mildly irritated, and exfoliation on top of that stings. If you wear a beard, that is fine too, just keep it clean and tell the therapist where you want the products applied. The facial works around facial hair without any problem.

Will my face look red after a facial?

Mild pinkness for a few hours is normal, especially after exfoliation and extractions. It usually settles by evening. That is why you should book three to four days before any event, never the morning of. If redness lasts beyond a day or feels hot, call the salon and describe what you are seeing.
