01 Start with a trial, not a booking

A trial is the single best way to judge a bridal salon, because it shows you the real work on your own face and hair instead of photos of someone else. Book the trial at least a month before the wedding, wear your outfit colour if you can, and bring the jewellery you plan to wear. If the salon will not do a trial, or rushes it, that tells you plenty.

During the trial, watch how the artist works. A good artist asks about your skin, checks for sensitivity, and builds the look step by step, checking with you as they go. The makeup should feel light on the skin and look right in daylight and in photos, not just under salon lights. Ask for photos in natural light before you leave.

02 Check the full bridal menu in one place

A wedding needs hair, makeup, mehndi, nails, and grooming for the family, and it is far easier when one salon covers all of it. The Stylist offers bridal makeup, party and engagement makeup, bridal hairstyles, hair colour, hair spa and head massage, manicure and pedicure, nail art, bridal mehndi, and mens grooming including beard shaping and haircuts for the groom and the family.

Ask whether the salon can handle the full wedding party, not just the bride, because mothers, sisters, and friends all need looks on the day. A salon that plans the whole party in one schedule keeps the morning calm, while a salon that can only do the bride leaves the rest of the family scrambling. Ask for a written plan with timings for each person.

03 Hygiene and honesty matter more than a fancy room

Bridal makeup uses brushes, sponges, and products on your skin, so hygiene is not a small detail. A good salon uses clean brushes, fresh sponges, and products that are in date, and the artist should be happy to show you. If the brushes look dirty or the products look old, walk away, because a skin reaction days before the wedding is the last thing you need.

Honesty matters too. A good artist tells you plainly if a look will not suit your skin tone or your outfit, and suggests what will work instead. An artist who agrees to everything is not helping you. Ask about the products, the wear time, and what happens if the makeup needs a touch-up during the function, and listen to how clearly the answers come.
