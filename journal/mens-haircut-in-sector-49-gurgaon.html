01 The two-minute consult that saves two months

Most bad men's cuts come from a bad consult, not bad scissors. We ask three things: how you style it at home, how often you will come back, and what you do for work. A cut needing daily product fails for a man who washes and goes.

Bring a photo, but expect the stylist to adjust it. Your texture, growth direction, and head shape decide how it translates. A good stylist tells you what will and will not work before the first cut, not after.

02 Fades, classics, and crops

Most men pick from three families. Fades go from short at the sides to length on top and need clean blending. Classics like the side part suit offices and weddings. Textured crops are the low-maintenance option: short, choppy, fine with minimal styling.

Growth patterns decide more than preference. A strong crown swirl or stubborn cowlick rules out some styles and improves others. We check how your hair grows first, because a cut that fights your growth looks messy by day three.

03 Beard to match the cut

A haircut and a beard are one look. The beard's neckline should meet the haircut's backline, and the cheek line should read as intentional. We shape to the jaw: fuller on a narrow chin, tighter on a rounder face.

Most men do well with a trim every two weeks between shapings. Ten minutes keeps the lines sharp. Ask us to show you your lines during the appointment and you can maintain them at home.

04 Hair that works at the office and the gym

Gurgaon men live in two uniforms: office and gym. A good cut handles both. Shorter sides stay neat under a helmet or after a workout; length on top gives you something to style for work. Tell us your routine and we cut for the harder of the two.

Product advice is part of the cut. Most men use too much or the wrong kind. A pea-sized amount of matte clay suits most Indian hair; shiny products look greasy by afternoon here. We show you the right amount once.

05 When to book your next cut

Short cuts need a refresh every three weeks, medium lengths every four to five. The test is simple: if you fight your hair in the morning, you waited too long. Regulars get faster appointments because the stylist already knows the cut.

Questions we hear at the salon

How often should men get a haircut?

Every three to four weeks for short cuts and fades, four to five for medium lengths. If you fight your hair in the morning, you waited too long. Regular visits are quicker since the stylist knows your cut.

What is the best haircut for Indian hair?

It depends on texture and growth pattern. Textured crops suit thick hair, tapered classics suit offices, fades suit men who want sharp sides. We check your growth direction before recommending.

Do you do beard trims with haircuts?

Yes. A cut plus beard shaping takes about 45 minutes, and we line the beard up with the haircut so the two read as one look. Maintenance trims between shapings take ten minutes.
