01 Shades that suit Diwali lights and family photos

Diwali photos are mostly taken indoors under warm yellow light, and that changes how a shade looks. Warm browns, caramel, copper, and chocolate shades catch that light well and look rich in photos. Cooler ash shades can look flat or grey under the same light, so they need more thought before you pick them.

If you have warm undertones, golden brown and honey highlights will light up your face without looking harsh. If your skin is cooler, a soft chocolate brown or a muted burgundy is often the safer first step into colour. Bring a photo of a look you like, and we will tell you honestly whether it suits your hair and your Diwali plans.

02 Root touch-up, global colour and highlights explained

A root touch-up covers regrowth only, usually every four to six weeks, and keeps your colour looking fresh without recolouring the whole head. It is the lowest-maintenance option for people who already have a colour they love.

Global colour changes the whole head to one shade. It gives the most even, polished result, but it needs regular root work. Highlights, by contrast, add lighter strands through your hair for depth and movement, and they grow out more softly, often lasting eight to twelve weeks between visits.

03 Patch test and hair health first

We start by checking your hair history: past colour, keratin or smoothening, and any sensitivity. If you are new to colour, we do a patch test first, because a reaction is rare but not worth risking. If your hair is dry or weak from past treatments, we may suggest a gentler shade or a conditioning treatment first.

Grey coverage needs a straight talk too. Full grey coverage needs a different product and more frequent touch-ups than a few blended highlights. Tell us how much grey you have and how often you are willing to come in, and we will pick the option that fits your routine.

04 What happens at your appointment

We apply the colour, check it at intervals, wash it out, and finish with a conditioning treatment and a blow dry. Global colour usually takes two to three hours, while highlights can take longer depending on how many foils you need. You will leave with clear aftercare advice and a realistic date for your next visit.

05 Aftercare that keeps colour fresh through Diwali

Use a shampoo made for coloured hair, wash with lukewarm water, and cut back on daily heat styling through the festive week. A colour-protecting mask once a week does more for your shade than any expensive serum used now and then.

Sun and hard Gurgaon water fade colour faster than most people expect. A hat on long sunny days and a shower filter, if you can manage one, both help your colour last. Book your Diwali slot early, because the week before Diwali is the busiest time of the year at our salon, and weekend appointments go first.

The honest test: if your colour looks natural in daylight, not just under salon lights, it is the right shade. Step outside and check in a mirror before you leave, and tell us straight away if anything looks off.

Questions we hear at the salon

How long before Diwali should I get my hair coloured?

A week to ten days before Diwali is ideal. The colour settles, any sharpness softens, and you still have time for a small fix if needed. Root touch-ups can be done closer to the day.

Which shade photographs best in Diwali lights?

Warm browns, caramel and copper catch warm festive light well and look natural in photos. Ash shades can look flat under yellow light, so tell us if most of your photos will be indoors.

Will hair colour damage my hair?

Any colour changes the hair a little, but the right product, the right developer strength, and proper aftercare keep damage low. We will not push a shade that needs heavy bleach if your hair is already weak.

Do you do a patch test before colour?

Yes, for first-time colour clients. It takes a few minutes and it rules out sensitivity before we apply colour to your full head.
