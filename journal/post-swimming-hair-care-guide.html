Post-Swimming Hair Care: Chlorine Is Not Your Friend

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 5 min read

You swim three mornings a week at the condo pool, and by the end of the month your hair feels like straw. It tangles faster, the colour looks dull, and the ends are rough no matter what conditioner you use. Chlorine does that. It strips the hair's natural oils and leaves the shaft dry and porous. The good news: a small pre-swim and post-swim routine prevents almost all of it.

01 What chlorine does to hair

Pool water is treated to kill bacteria, and that same chemistry is rough on hair. Chlorine strips the natural oils that keep the shaft smooth, leaving hair dry, tangled and prone to breakage. On lightened or highlighted hair it can also leave a greenish tint, which is actually mineral deposit binding to porous hair, not the chlorine itself.

The damage is cumulative. One swim barely registers. Three swims a week for a month without any protection, and the hair tells the story: rough texture, dull colour, ends that will not behave. Swimmers who also deal with Gurugram's hard water at home get a double dose of mineral build-up, which is why pool regulars here notice the change faster.

02 Before you get in

The most effective step happens before the swim, not after. Dry hair soaks up pool water like a sponge. Wet hair absorbs far less.

  • Soak your hair in clean shower water first. Every pool has showers for a reason. Wet the hair fully, root to tip, before you enter.
  • Add a leave-in conditioner or a little oil. On wet hair, a light layer of conditioner or coconut oil creates a barrier that further reduces how much pool water the shaft takes in.
  • Wear a swim cap if you can stand it. It will not keep hair bone dry, but it cuts exposure dramatically. For daily swimmers it is the single biggest difference-maker.
  • Tie long hair up. A bun or braid under the cap keeps the lengths from dragging through the water the whole session.

03 The minute you get out

Rinse immediately. Not after coffee with friends, not when you get home. The longer chlorinated water dries on the hair, the more it deposits. A thorough rinse under the pool shower takes two minutes and removes most of the problem.

Then shampoo at home with a proper wash. If you swim several times a week, use a clarifying or swimmers' shampoo once a week to lift chlorine and mineral build-up that regular shampoo leaves behind. Follow every swim-day wash with conditioner on the lengths, because pool hair is thirsty hair.

The order that works: wet hair before, rinse the moment after, clarifying shampoo weekly. Skip the first step and the other two are damage control. Do the first step and the rest is easy.

04 Undoing the damage already done

If your hair already feels like straw, start with a clarifying wash to strip the build-up, then a deep conditioning mask on the lengths. One round usually restores the texture noticeably. If the hair is still rough after that, the ends may be too far gone, and a trim will do more than any product. Our guide to dry, frizzy ends explains how to tell.

For regular swimmers, a professional reset every couple of months keeps things manageable. A hair spa session deep-cleans mineral and chlorine build-up and rehydrates the lengths properly. Questions we hear at the salon

Should I wash my hair right after swimming?

Yes, rinse immediately at the pool and shampoo properly at home. The longer chlorinated water sits and dries on the hair, the more it strips oils and deposits minerals. A two-minute rinse under the pool shower removes most of the problem before it sets in, and a full wash finishes the job.

Can I just use regular shampoo after swimming?

For occasional swims, yes. If you swim several times a week, add a clarifying or swimmers' shampoo once a week. Regular shampoo does not fully lift chlorine and mineral build-up, which accumulates over weeks and leaves hair rough and dull. Always follow with conditioner on the lengths.

Why does my blonde or highlighted hair turn green in pools?

The greenish tint comes from minerals, especially copper, in pool water binding to porous lightened hair, not from chlorine itself. Wetting hair with clean water before swimming and wearing a swim cap reduce it. A clarifying shampoo usually lifts mild tint; stubborn cases may need a salon treatment.

Does a swim cap really protect hair?

It helps a lot, though it will not keep hair completely dry. A cap cuts the hair's exposure to chlorinated water dramatically, especially combined with wetting the hair first. For daily swimmers it is the single biggest difference-maker, more than any post-swim product.
