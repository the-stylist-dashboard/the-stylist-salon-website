01 How to brief your stylist so you get the cut you want

The best haircuts start with a clear brief. Tell your stylist how you wear your hair on a normal day, how much time you spend styling it, and what you hated about your last cut. That matters more than any celebrity photo.

Bring two or three photos of cuts you like, and also one you do not want. Photos show length, layers, and fringe in a way words often miss, and they help us spot at once if a style needs daily heat styling you will not do.

Be honest about your hair too. Fine hair, thick hair, wavy hair, and curly hair all behave differently after a cut, and a good stylist will adjust the plan to your texture instead of copying a photo exactly.

02 Cuts that suit Indian hair and Gurgaon weather

Long layers are the safest all-rounder for Indian hair. They remove weight, add movement, and grow out gracefully, which matters when humidity and hard water already make styling harder.

A bob works well if you like a neat, polished look and do not mind a trim every six to eight weeks. For wavy or curly hair, a longer cut with soft layers usually sits better than a very short, sharp shape.

03 What happens during your haircut appointment

We wash your hair first, then cut it damp so we can see how it falls naturally. For curly hair, we may check the shape dry as well, because curls spring up shorter than they look when wet.

Your stylist will talk through length before cutting anything major. If you ask for three inches off, we will show you where that falls before we start, so there are no surprises in the mirror.

The appointment usually takes forty five minutes to an hour, including wash and blow dry. If you want colour or a hair spa on the same visit, mention it when you book on 81781 59945 so we allow enough time.

04 Layers, steps, and texturing: what the words mean

Layers are shorter pieces cut through the hair to add movement and remove bulk. Steps are more visible, graduated lengths, and they suit people who want a clearly shaped, bouncy finish.

05 How to keep your cut looking good between visits

Trim every six to eight weeks if you wear a short cut, and every eight to twelve weeks for long hair. Waiting too long lets split ends travel up the hair and makes the next cut shorter than you planned.

Use a mild shampoo, a conditioner on the lengths only, and a little serum on the ends after washing. Skip daily heat styling where you can, and let your hair air dry on days you stay home.

Questions we hear at the salon

How often should I get a haircut?

Every six to eight weeks for short cuts, and every eight to twelve weeks for long hair. Regular trims stop split ends and keep the shape you paid for.

Should I wash my hair before my haircut?

No need. We wash your hair at the salon before cutting, so come as you are and let us handle the wash and the cut in one visit.

Can I get a haircut and hair colour on the same day?

Yes, and it is often the better order to cut first, then colour. Tell us when you book so we set aside enough time for both.

What if I do not like my haircut?

Tell us within a few days and come back. Small fixes like evening out a layer or softening a fringe are usually quick, and we would rather fix it than have you unhappy.
