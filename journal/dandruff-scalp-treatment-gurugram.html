Dandruff in NCR: When a Salon Treatment Beats Home Remedies

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 27 September 2026 · 7 min read

Dandruff in NCR is not just a shampoo problem. Hard water, winter dryness, summer sweat and pollution stack onto the scalp until the drugstore anti-dandruff bottle stops working. At that point the choice is a professional scalp treatment or the dermatologist's chair. Here is how to tell which one you need.

The short answer

Most ordinary dandruff improves with a professional scalp treatment: exfoliation, deep cleansing and a targeted masque, usually showing results in one to two sessions. Home remedies maintain but rarely rescue a real flare-up. If flakes are thick and yellowish, or itching breaks the skin, see a dermatologist; that is likely seborrheic dermatitis, not ordinary dandruff.

01 Why NCR scalps flake more

Hard water. Mineral film coats the scalp just as it coats the hair, trapping dead skin that should rinse away. Our hard water guide covers the full picture.

Winter dryness. Cold air plus indoor heating dries the scalp like it dries everything else, and dry scalps shed dry flakes.

Summer sweat and pollution. Sweat, dust and helmet wear create the warm, damp scalp environment where the yeast behind oily dandruff thrives.

Stress. The usual suspect behind sudden flare-ups with no routine change.

02 What a salon scalp treatment does

Assessment comes first, because dry flakes, oily flakes and redness need different protocols. Then: exfoliation with a scalp scrub or peeling masque to lift what is stuck; a proper deep cleanse; a targeted masque, anti-fungal for oily dandruff, calming for dry; steam to help absorption; and a rinse-routine reset so you stop recreating the problem at home.

One session calms most mild cases. Stubborn or recurring dandruff usually needs two or three sessions spaced a fortnight apart, plus the home routine changes we will walk you through.

03 Home remedies, rated

Anti-dandruff shampoo: works, if you massage it into the scalp and leave it three to five minutes. Almost nobody does; most people rinse it off in thirty seconds and blame the shampoo.

Oiling: helps dry flakes, feeds fungal dandruff. Know your flake type before you pour.

Curd and lemon: mild, inconsistent, harmless to try.

Apple cider vinegar rinse: a decent clarifier for buildup between treatments.

The pattern: home care maintains a healthy scalp well. It rarely rescues a scalp that is already flaring.

04 When it is not dandruff

Thick yellowish scales, intense itching, redness, or flakes spreading beyond the scalp to eyebrows and ears: that is likely seborrheic dermatitis or psoriasis, and no salon treatment should be sold as its cure. See a dermatologist.

We say this in the consultation too. A salon that tries to treat what is clearly a medical condition is a salon to leave.

The shampoo mistake almost everyone makes: anti-dandruff shampoo applied to the hair lengths instead of the scalp. It is a scalp treatment. Massage it into the scalp, leave it three minutes, rinse. The lengths only need the runoff.

Dandruff and Scalp Treatment questions, answered

How many scalp treatment sessions will I need?

Mild dandruff often improves after one session, with a second a fortnight later to consolidate. Stubborn or recurring cases usually need three sessions plus changes to the home routine.

Can dandruff cause hair fall?

Indirectly, yes. Constant itching and scratching damages hair at the root, and heavy buildup weakens the scalp environment. Clearing the dandruff usually slows the shedding within weeks.

Is dandruff contagious?

No. Dandruff is your own scalp's response to yeast that already lives on everyone's skin, combined with dryness or sensitivity. You cannot catch it from anyone or give it to anyone.

Should I oil my hair if I have dandruff?

Only for the dry-flake kind. With oily or yellowish flakes, oiling feeds the yeast behind the problem and usually makes it worse. Get the flake type assessed before deciding.
