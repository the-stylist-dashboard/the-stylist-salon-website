01 Step one: shortlist two or three salons

Start by shortlisting two or three salons that do bridal work regularly, not every salon in the area. Look for a salon with a full bridal menu, hair, makeup, mehndi, nails, and grooming for the family, because one salon covering all of it keeps the wedding morning simple. Ask friends who married recently which salon they used, and read the reviews with care, looking for comments about trials and wedding mornings, not just about haircuts.

Call each salon on your shortlist and ask the same questions: do they do trials, how long does a trial take, can they handle the whole wedding party, and what are their timings for a wedding morning. The answers will tell you a lot. A salon that cannot answer plainly about trials or timings is not the right pick, while a salon that talks through the plan clearly is worth visiting.

02 Step two: visit the salon before you book

Visit your favourite salon in person before you book anything, because a visit shows you what no photo can. Look at the workstations, the tools, and the brushes, and check whether the place feels clean and calm. Talk to the artist who would do your makeup, not just the person at the desk, and ask about your skin, your outfit, and your functions, so you can judge how well they listen.

A good artist asks about your skin, checks for sensitivity, and talks about what will suit you, while a careless one agrees to everything without asking a single question. Ask to see the products they use, and check that they are in date. If the brushes look dirty or the products look old, walk away, because a skin reaction days before the wedding is the last thing you need.

Walk away from a salon that will not do a trial, or rushes it, because that tells you plenty about how the wedding day will go. Walk away from a salon that cannot handle the whole wedding party, because mothers, sisters, and friends all need looks on the day, and a salon that can only do the bride leaves the rest of the family scrambling. Ask for a written plan with timings for each person and each function, and walk away if the salon will not give you one.

03 Step three: book the trial, then judge the result

Book the trial four to six weeks before the wedding, and wear your outfit colour if you can, so the artist can see the real look. Bring the jewellery you plan to wear, and photos of looks you like, but keep them as a guide, not a demand, because the artist will adapt the look to your face and your skin. Take photos in daylight before you leave, because salon lights can flatter a look that daylight does not.

Wear the trial look for a few hours to see how it holds and how it feels, because a look that is lovely for ten minutes may feel heavy after two hours. The makeup should feel light on the skin and look right in daylight and in photos. If the look slips, pulls, or feels wrong, tell the artist, because a small change can fix it, and the trial is the time to find these problems, not the wedding day.
