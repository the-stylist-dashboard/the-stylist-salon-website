Groom Packages: What Men Actually Need Before the Wedding

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

The bride has a forty-item checklist and a coordinator. You have a wedding in six weeks and a vague plan to "get a haircut before it." Meanwhile the photographer will take four hundred close-ups of your face, and every one of them lasts forever. Groom prep is not about becoming someone else for the photos. It is about showing up as the best-maintained version of yourself, on a timeline that leaves nothing to the last week.

01 What groom packages usually include

Most groom packages bundle the same core items: a haircut, beard shaping or a clean shave, a facial or de-tan, a manicure and pedicure, and sometimes a head massage or body polish on top. Salons package them because bundling is convenient, not because every item is essential.

The core value sits in the first four. Clean hair, a deliberate beard, even-toned skin, and presentable hands cover ninety percent of what the camera sees. The extras are comfort, nice to have, easy to skip if the budget or the calendar is tight.

02 The timeline that actually works

Timing beats spending. Here is the schedule that avoids every common groom disaster:

  • Four weeks out: the trial haircut and beard shape. Try the exact look you want for the wedding day. Photos lie; the mirror does not. If it does not suit you, there is still time to adjust.
  • Two weeks out: the facial or de-tan. This leaves room for any redness to settle and for the skin to look its best on the day. Our bridal skincare timeline runs through the same logic, and most of it applies to grooms.
  • Three to four days before: the final haircut and beard shaping. Close enough to look fresh, far enough that nothing looks freshly-cut and stiff.
  • The final week: nothing new. No new products, no experimental treatments, no first-time anything on your face or head.

03 Hair: the trial run rule

Never debut a new hairstyle at your own wedding. The trial cut four weeks out exists so you can live with the look, see it in photos, and walk it back if needed. Most grooms do best with a sharper, cleaner version of their usual cut rather than a dramatic change. Familiar, but the best version of familiar.

If you are covering grey, that trial appointment is also when the colour gets tested. For the full thinking on this, our men's haircut guide covers how cuts match faces, which matters more than usual when the photos are forever.

04 Skin: what grooms actually need

Two things: even tone and clean texture. A de-tan handles the helmet strap lines, watch tans, and collar lines that Indian weddings photograph mercilessly. A facial handles the clogged pores and dullness that close-up photography exaggerates. Many grooms need both, done in the same sitting two weeks out.

What you do not need: a ten-step routine you will abandon. Sunscreen in the mornings between now and the wedding does more for your skin than any product haul. Start that habit the day you book the package.

05 The details people notice

Hands show up in the ring exchange photos, in the garland photos, everywhere. A manicure is not vanity; it is photo insurance, and it takes thirty minutes. Same logic for a pedicure if you are wearing traditional footwear. Nails trimmed, cuticles clean, done.

Eyebrows deserve one line: clean up the obvious strays, do not reshape. Overdone groom eyebrows are visible from the back row. And sleep the week before the wedding. No facial fixes three nights of sangeet-party exhaustion, though a good one can hide one.

Questions we hear at the salon

When should a groom start his wedding prep?

Start four weeks out with a trial haircut and beard shape, so there are no surprises. Do the facial or de-tan about two weeks before, which leaves time for any redness to settle. The final haircut and beard shaping happen three to four days before the wedding. Nothing new goes on the face or head in the final week.

What is usually included in a groom package?

Most groom packages bundle a haircut, beard shaping, facial or de-tan, manicure and pedicure, and sometimes a head massage or body polish. The core value is in the first four. Extras like body polishing are optional comfort, not essentials. Ask for an itemised list before booking so you can drop what you do not need.

Should the groom get a facial before the wedding?

Yes, if timed right. A facial two weeks before the wedding deep-cleans the skin and evens out the texture that shows up in close-up photos. Never book it in the final week, since skin occasionally needs a day or two to settle. A de-tan in the same sitting handles tan lines from outdoor functions leading up to the day.
