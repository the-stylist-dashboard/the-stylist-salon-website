01 Pick a shade for the festive week, not just for one night

Diwali week means several functions, different outfits, and many photos in different light. Pick a shade that works across those outfits, not just with one lehenga or saree. Warm browns, soft coppers, and rich chocolate tones suit most festive outfits, while very light or very bold shades need more upkeep and can clash with some colours.

Bring photos of your main outfits, and tell your stylist which function matters most. A shade that looks great under indoor lights can look different in daylight, so ask to see the shade in natural light before you agree. Your stylist should also check your past colour work, because old colour and bleach change how a new shade takes.

02 Global colour, highlights, or balayage for Diwali

Global colour gives an even, clear change across the whole head, and it suits clients who want a fresh, obvious festive look. Highlights add lighter pieces for depth and shine, and they suit clients who want brightness without a full change. Balayage gives a softer, hand-painted finish that grows out gently, and it suits clients who want a lower upkeep option after the festive week.

For Diwali, think about the week after too. A colour that needs a root touch-up within two weeks will add another salon visit right after the festival. Ask how each option grows out, and pick the one you can maintain. A colour that still looks good two weeks later is a better Diwali choice than one that only looks good on day one.

03 Timing, patch test, and the week before Diwali

Book colour in the week before Diwali on 8 November 2026, with a gap of three to five days before your main function. That gap lets the shade settle and gives time for a quick tone correction if needed. Colour on the day of a function is a bad plan, because the shade has not settled and the hair may still feel dry from the process.

Tell your stylist about any scalp sensitivity or past reaction to colour, and ask about a patch test if you have reacted before. Also tell them about recent keratin, smoothening, or henna work, because those change how colour behaves. A short honest history at consultation prevents a long correction later.

04 Aftercare through the festive week

After colour, wash less often in the first days, use lukewarm water, and protect the hair from heat tools when you style for functions. A gentle wash routine keeps the shade richer for longer, while very hot water and daily heat styling fade it faster. Ask your stylist to write down the aftercare for your shade, because global colour and balayage need slightly different care.
