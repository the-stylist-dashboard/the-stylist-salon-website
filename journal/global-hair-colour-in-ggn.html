01 What global colour is, and who it suits

Global colour means the whole head is coloured in one shade, from roots to ends, so the result is even and clear. It suits clients who want a full change, such as a rich brown, a deep black, or a warm copper, and it suits Diwali and wedding functions where the hair needs to look polished in photos. It also suits clients who want to cover grey evenly, with one shade across the whole head.

Global colour needs more upkeep than highlights or balayage, because regrowth shows as a clear line at the roots. If you want a softer change that grows out gently, highlights or balayage may suit better. Tell your stylist about your plans after the function, because a shade picked for one evening should still suit the weeks after it, not just the night itself.

02 Picking a shade for your skin tone

Warm skin tones usually suit warm browns, copper, caramel, and golden shades, while cool skin tones usually suit ash browns, cool black, and muted burgundy. Bring a photo of your outfit, because the colour should sit well with the outfit shade, not fight it. A deep red outfit and a bright copper colour can clash, while a softer brown keeps the focus on you.

If you are unsure, ask for a shade that is one or two tones away from your natural colour, not a drastic change. A gentle change looks polished and grows out gracefully, while a very light shade on dark hair needs more upkeep and more visits. Your stylist can show you how a shade will look in daylight, not just under salon lights, so you can decide with a clear head.

03 Timing global colour before a function

Book global colour three to five days before your main function, so the shade settles and there is time for a small correction if needed. Colour done on the day of a function leaves no time for a fix if the shade is not right. A gap of a few days also lets you wash once and see how the colour truly looks.

Avoid a major colour change in the final two days before a function, especially if you have never coloured your hair before. A first colour needs a patch test and a consultation, and both need time. If your hair is dry or damaged, a hair spa a week before the colour helps the shade take evenly and last longer, and a spa a few days after helps the shade settle.
