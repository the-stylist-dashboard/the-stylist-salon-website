Nail extensions promise perfect hands in one sitting, and they deliver, when they are done right. When they are done wrong, you get lifting edges, painful breaks, and natural nails that take weeks to recover. We do extensions at The Stylist regularly, and we would rather talk you out of them than do a set that is not right for you. Here is the straight version of what you need to know.

01 Gel vs acrylic: the simple version

Gel extensions are built with a gel that cures hard under a UV or LED lamp. They look natural, feel light, and give a smooth finish that does not need much buffing. Most clients prefer gel for everyday wear because it is comfortable and the shine lasts.

Acrylic extensions mix a liquid and a powder that hardens in air. They are harder and stronger than gel, which suits long lengths and hands that take a beating. The trade-off is a stronger smell during application and a slightly thicker look. For a first set, or for office-friendly nails, gel is the easier starting point. For very long or dramatic shapes, acrylic handles the stress better.

02 Choosing length and shape

Be honest about your hands. If you type all day, handle kids, or do housework without gloves, short to medium length is the sensible choice. Long extensions on rough hands break at the stress point, and a break hurts and costs you a repair visit.

Shape follows fingers. Almond and oval shapes flatter most hands and look elegant without trying too hard. Coffin and stiletto shapes photograph beautifully but need length to carry them, and they catch on everything. Square and squoval suit longer fingers and give a clean, modern edge. Your nail technician should look at your fingers and be honest about what will survive your daily routine, not just what looks good on a poster.

03 How long they last, and when to refill

A good set lasts 3 to 4 weeks. Gel usually holds well for 2 to 3 weeks before growth at the cuticle starts showing. Acrylic can go a little longer. How you treat your hands matters more than the material. Gentle use stretches the life, rough use cuts it short.

Book a refill every 2 to 3 weeks. The refill fills the growth gap, checks for lifting, and replaces any weakened tips. If you see lifting at the edges, come in sooner. Lifting lets water and soap get underneath the extension, and trapped moisture is how problems start. Waiting too long also shifts the weight as the nail grows out, which stresses the natural nail and makes breaks more likely.

The removal rule: never peel or prise extensions off at home. Peeling lifts layers of your natural nail with it, and that is where most extension damage comes from. A salon soak-off takes 20 minutes and keeps your nails intact. It is the cheapest insurance your nails have.

04 Aftercare that actually matters

Keep them dry for the first few hours after application so the bond sets properly. After that, normal hand washing is fine, but do not soak your nails for long periods. Wear gloves for cleaning, dishwashing, and anything with harsh chemicals. Oil your cuticles daily, a drop of cuticle oil massaged in keeps the extension flexible and the natural nail underneath healthy.

Do not use your nails as tools. No opening cans, scraping stickers, or prying things. That is the number one cause of breaks we see. If an extension chips or cracks, do not file it aggressively or clip it short. Call the salon, because a quick repair is cheap and painless. A bad DIY fix usually means we have to redo the whole nail.

05 Who should skip extensions

Extensions are not for everyone, and a salon that says otherwise is not being honest. Skip them if you have an active nail infection, damaged or very thin nails that need to heal, or a habit of biting or picking that will destroy the set in days. Skin allergies to acrylates are another reason to pause and talk to the technician first.

If extensions are not right for you yet, a proper manicure still gives you clean, shaped, polished nails without the commitment. Our manicure and pedicure guide covers that. And if you are prepping for a big function and want the full treatment, a facial a week before leaves your skin glowing for the photos too.

Questions we hear at the salon

How long do nail extensions last?

Most nail extensions last 3 to 4 weeks before they need a refill. Gel extensions usually look good for 2 to 3 weeks, acrylic a bit longer. How you treat your hands matters more than the material: gentle use stretches the life, rough use cuts it short.

Are gel or acrylic extensions better?

Gel extensions look more natural and feel lighter, and they cure under a UV or LED lamp. Acrylics are harder and stronger, which suits long lengths or rough hands, but they have a stronger smell during application. For everyday wear, most clients prefer gel for comfort and a natural finish.

Do nail extensions damage natural nails?

Done well and removed properly, extensions do minimal damage. Most damage comes from peeling or prising extensions off at home, which lifts layers of the natural nail. Always have them soaked off at the salon, and give your nails a short break between long-wearing sets if they feel thin.

When should I book a refill?

Book a refill every 2 to 3 weeks, or as soon as you see lifting at the edges. Lifting lets water and soap get underneath, which can cause lifting infections. Waiting too long also stresses the natural nail as the extension grows out and shifts the weight.
