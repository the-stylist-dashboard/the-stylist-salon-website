Feet take a beating in Gurgaon. Dust, heat, closed shoes on some days and open sandals on others, and long hours on your feet all leave their mark. A good pedicure is not just painted nails, it is proper foot care: clean nails, smooth heels, and skin that feels comfortable again. Here is what a good pedicure should include, how often you need one, and how to keep the result going.

01 What a good pedicure actually includes

A proper pedicure starts with a warm soak to soften the skin, then nails are trimmed straight across and shaped, and cuticles are pushed back gently, never cut aggressively. Hard skin on the heels and soles is filed down, calluses are treated carefully, and the feet are exfoliated and massaged with moisturiser. Only then comes polish, if you want it. If a salon skips straight to paint without the foot care, you are paying for colour, not a pedicure. Watch for clean tools too: instruments should be sanitised in front of you or taken from sealed packs.

02 Regular polish or gel: which to pick

Regular polish is cheaper, easy to remove, and fine if you like changing colours often. It chips faster, usually within a week on toes that see a lot of wear. Gel polish lasts two to three weeks without chipping and suits holidays, weddings, and anyone who does not want to think about their toes for a while. Gel needs proper removal, though: peeling it off damages the nail underneath. Pick regular polish if you change your mind often, and gel if you want a low-maintenance result. Either way, a base coat and a top coat matter, so do not let anyone skip them.

03 Cracked heels and calluses need patience

Cracked heels do not heal in one sitting, no matter what a salon promises. A good pedicure softens them and files down the worst of the hard skin, but deep cracks need regular care over several weeks. Moisturise daily with a thick foot cream, wear socks at night after applying it, and avoid walking barefoot on rough floors. Calluses form where shoes rub, so check your footwear too: tight sandals undo pedicure work fast. If cracks bleed or look infected, see a doctor before booking a pedicure. A responsible salon will say the same.

04 How often should you get a pedicure

Every three to four weeks works for most people. That is often enough to keep nails tidy, heels smooth, and polish fresh without overdoing it. If you wear closed shoes daily or walk a lot, you may need the shorter end of that range. In summer, when feet are on show in sandals, people often come more often for the polish alone. Between visits, trim nails straight across, moisturise after showers, and keep feet dry inside shoes to avoid fungal trouble. A pedicure every month plus basic home care beats an expensive pedicure twice a year.

Questions we hear at the salon

How long does a pedicure take?

A standard pedicure takes 45 minutes to an hour. Gel polish adds 15 to 20 minutes. Tell the salon if you are in a hurry so they can plan the appointment properly.

Is gel polish bad for toenails?

Gel itself is not the problem, bad removal is. Peeling gel off takes nail layers with it. Always have gel removed properly at the salon, and give nails a short break between gel sets.

Can a pedicure fix cracked heels completely?

One pedicure softens cracks and smooths hard skin, but deep cracks need several weeks of regular care and daily moisturising. Be wary of any salon that promises a one-visit cure.
