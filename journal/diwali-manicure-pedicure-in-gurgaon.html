01 What a combo session includes

A manicure shapes the nails, tidies cuticles, and finishes with polish, while a pedicure adds a soak, a scrub for rough skin, and care for heels. A short hand and foot massage is part of the session and helps you relax before a busy festival week.

Tell the team if you have sensitive skin, a broken nail, or a polish allergy. That detail changes the products used and keeps a festive service from turning into a sore week after Diwali.

02 Gel polish or regular polish

Regular polish suits clients who like to change shade often and who do not mind a touch-up. Gel polish lasts longer and suits a full festival week, but it needs proper removal, so do not peel it off at home.

If mehndi is also planned, say so when you book. Manicure work usually comes before mehndi, so the mehndi design is not disturbed, and the team can plan the order across two days.

03 When to book before Diwali

Book the manicure pedicure one to two days before Diwali on 8 November 2026, so polish is fresh on the day but has had a night to harden fully. Book earlier in the week if you also need hair colour, a facial, or mehndi, so the week is spread out.

Diwali week slots go early. A call now holds your slot, while a walk-in during that week may mean waiting or missing the shade you wanted.

04 Keeping nails good through the festival

Wear gloves for heavy cleaning work during Diwali prep, and moisturise hands and feet each night. Polish chips fastest from household work, not from normal wear, so a little care keeps the finish tidy.

If a nail chips, do not peel the rest of the polish. A quick salon fix keeps the nail healthy and the set looking even for the remaining functions.

05 Choosing a shade for Diwali

Pick your polish shade with your Diwali outfit in mind. Classic reds and deep maroons suit most festive outfits, while softer nudes suit a lighter daytime look. If you are unsure, ask the team to hold two shades against your hand before deciding.

Hands and feet do not have to match exactly. Many clients pick a deeper shade for toes and a softer shade for hands, or the same shade for both. Either choice works, as long as it suits the outfit you will wear most.

If mehndi is also planned, pick the polish after the mehndi discussion, not before. The mehndi colour and the polish shade sit side by side on the hands, and planning them together keeps the combination looking deliberate.

For Diwali night itself, a top coat on the day keeps the polish glossy through greetings, cooking, and photos. Ask the team about a top coat refresh if your polish was done two days before, so the finish still looks fresh on the festival night.
