Thinking of colouring your hair in Sector 57? Here is the honest version of what we tell clients across the chair: which shades work, what they cost, and how to keep your hair healthy through it.

01 Start with your undertone, not the trend

Trends come and go, but your skin's undertone decides which shades suit you. Warm undertones (veins look greenish) suit copper, caramel and golden browns. Cool undertones (veins look bluish) suit ash browns, espresso and burgundy reds.

For a first colour, stay within one or two levels of your natural shade. It looks expensive, grows out gracefully, and you can always go bolder next time.

02 Global colour, highlights or balayage

  • Global colour: one shade from roots to ends. Best for full grey coverage or a complete change, with a root touch-up every four to six weeks.
  • Highlights: lighter strands woven through for depth, with softer regrowth than global colour.
  • Balayage: hand-painted colour starting a few inches from the roots. The lowest-maintenance option, with a grow-out you barely notice.

If your main goal is hiding greys, global colour is the answer. If you want dimension and easy upkeep, balayage wins.

03 What colouring really costs

Prices move with hair length and the brand used, so treat any quote that ignores your length as a red flag. A root touch-up costs the least, full global colour on long hair costs more, and balayage or fashion shades cost more still. Always ask what the quote includes: pre-lightening, toner, the gloss, and aftercare shampoo. A toner billed separately afterwards is a pricing trick, not a service.

We quote before we start, with everything named. You approve the number, then we mix the colour. No surprises at the counter.

04 Protecting your hair during colour

Modern colouring includes a bond-protecting additive mixed right into the colour. It costs the salon a little extra, but it keeps the hair's inner structure intact through the chemical process. Ask whether your salon uses one. If the answer is a blank stare, think twice.

Two rules from experience. Never colour over box dye without telling your stylist; it reacts unpredictably with salon colour. And if your hair is already snapping, a good stylist will refuse to colour it and suggest a repair-focused treatment first.

The patch test rule: first-time colour clients get a small patch test 48 hours before the appointment. It takes two minutes and rules out allergies. Any salon that skips it is cutting a corner on your safety.

05 Keeping the colour looking fresh in Gurgaon

Gurgaon is hard on colour. The sun fades reds fast and hard water dulls blondes. Use a sulphate-free, colour-safe shampoo and rinse with cool water. A colour-depositing conditioner once a week keeps the tone alive, and blondes should use a purple shampoo every third wash.

Book a gloss or toner refresh at the six-to-eight-week mark. It costs far less than a full recolour and makes the existing colour look newly done.

Questions we hear at the salon

How do I choose a hair colour shade that suits me?

Look at your skin's undertone first. Warm undertones suit copper, caramel and golden browns; cool undertones suit ash browns and espresso. For a first colour, stay one or two levels from your natural shade. Our stylists in Sector 52 consult on shade before every colour service.

Will colouring damage my hair?

Colouring done properly with a bond-protecting product does far less damage than people fear. Trouble starts with repeated bleach without recovery time, or box dye over already-coloured hair. A stylist checks your hair first and will tell you honestly if it needs a break.

How long does hair colour last?

Permanent colour needs a root touch-up every four to six weeks. Highlights and balayage grow out softly and last three to four months. Semi-permanent washes out in four to eight washes. Gurgaon's sun and hard water fade colour faster, so use colour-safe shampoo.
