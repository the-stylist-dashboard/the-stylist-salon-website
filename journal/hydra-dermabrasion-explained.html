Hydra-Dermabrasion, Explained in Plain Language

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

A client sat in our chair last month and asked, "So is this like a facial with a vacuum cleaner?" Honestly, that is not a bad description. Hydra-dermabrasion has become the most asked-about treatment on our menu, and also the most misunderstood. Here is what the session actually involves, who usually books it, and the downsides nobody puts on the poster.

01 The session, step by step

You lie back and the therapist runs a handheld device over your face in passes. The tip lightly exfoliates while gentle suction lifts away loosened dead skin and debris, and serums are applied as it goes. It feels cool and wet, a little like a tiny vacuum with water, and most people find it comfortable. A full session takes 45 to 60 minutes, and there is usually no redness worth hiding afterwards.

After the device passes, the therapist finishes with moisturiser and sunscreen, same as any facial. You walk out looking fresh rather than flushed, which is exactly why people book it before events.

02 Who usually books it

Three kinds of clients keep this chair busy. People prepping for an event who want visible freshness without downtime. People who dislike the heavy manual extraction of a classic facial. And regulars who like the deep-clean feeling of the suction passes. It also suits men trying their first serious skin service, since it is machine-led and straightforward. Pricing varies with the serums used, so call the salon and ask rather than guessing from a menu.

03 What it is not

It is not a one-sitting fix for deep-set concerns, and it does not replace regular skincare habits. Anyone promising dramatic permanent change from a single session is selling, not advising. It is also not painless for everyone; the suction around the nose and chin can feel odd if your skin is sensitive there. And like any exfoliating treatment, your skin will be more sun-aware afterwards, so sunscreen is not optional.

04 Before and after: the practical bits

Come with a clean face and skip strong exfoliating products for a couple of days before. After the session, keep it simple: gentle cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen, and no heavy makeup for the rest of the day if you can manage it. Clients usually notice skin feeling smoother and looking fresher for a week or so.

Questions we hear at the salon

Does hydra-dermabrasion hurt?

Most people find it comfortable: cool, wet, with a mild suction feeling. The passes around the nose and chin can feel strange if your skin is sensitive there, but it is rarely described as painful. It is gentler than heavy manual extraction for most clients.

How is it different from a regular facial?

A regular facial relies on manual cleansing, massage and masks, while hydra-dermabrasion uses a device that exfoliates, suctions and infuses serums in one pass. Sessions feel more methodical and usually involve less redness afterwards, which is why event-goers like it.

Can I wear makeup right after hydra-dermabrasion?

You can, but waiting a few hours is better. Your skin has just been exfoliated and is more receptive, so heavy makeup sits oddly and can clog freshly cleared pores. If you must, keep it light and remove it gently that night.

Is hydra-dermabrasion suitable for sensitive skin?

Often yes, since there is no harsh scrubbing and the therapist controls the suction strength. But if your skin is currently reactive, tight or flaky, postpone the session. Exfoliating an already stressed barrier is what causes bad reactions, so wait for calm skin.
