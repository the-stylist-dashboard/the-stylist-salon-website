01 What rebonding does

Rebonding is the strongest chemical straightening, and it is the one to pick when you want truly straight hair that stays straight for months. The stylist applies a cream that softens the bonds in the hair, irons the hair straight section by section, then applies a neutraliser that locks the new straight shape in place. The treated length stays straight until it is cut off, and only the new growth comes in wavy.

The treatment suits thick, wavy, or curly hair best, because that hair holds the straight finish well. Fine hair can be rebonded too, but it needs a gentler approach and a careful stylist, because fine hair is easier to damage. Hair that is already very damaged, bleached, or over-treated may not be right for rebonding, and the stylist will say so plainly after the hair check.

02 The session and the aftercare

A rebonding session takes four to five hours, so plan your whole day around it. The stylist washes the hair, applies the rebonding cream section by section, lets it work, then irons the hair straight and applies a neutraliser. After that, the hair is washed again and dried straight, so you leave with the finished look on the same day and you can see the result right away.

Bring a photo of the finish you want, and tell the stylist about any colour or chemical work your hair has had in the last few months. Colour and rebonding can be done together, but the order matters, and the stylist will plan it so your hair stays healthy. If your hair is very dry, the stylist may suggest a hair spa first to build strength before the chemical step.

The first three days after rebonding matter most. Do not wash the hair, tie it up tightly, or tuck it behind your ears, because the hair is still setting and a crease can stay in. Use a sulphate-free shampoo and conditioner, and wash less often than before, because rebonded hair stays cleaner for longer and needs less washing.

A hair spa once a month keeps rebonded hair soft and shiny, because the treatment dries the hair over time. Avoid swimming in chlorinated water without a cap, and use a heat protectant if you use a dryer often. With this care, the straight finish stays neat for months, and a touch-up on the regrowth keeps it looking even.

03 Rebonding or smoothening: which suits you

Rebonding gives a pin-straight finish that lasts till the hair grows out, while smoothening gives a softer, more natural straightness that relaxes over three to four months. If you want your hair dead straight every day and you are happy to manage the regrowth, rebonding is the better pick. If you want a softer finish that still moves naturally, smoothening may suit you better.

Both treatments need the same aftercare: no washing for three days, a sulphate-free shampoo, and a hair spa once a month. The regrowth line is more visible with rebonding, because the treated hair stays straight while the new growth comes in wavy, so plan a touch-up every four to six months if you want to keep the finish even.
