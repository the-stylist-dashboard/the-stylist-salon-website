01 What threading is and why so many people prefer it

Threading uses a twisted cotton thread to pull out unwanted hair from the root. It is precise, it needs no wax or chemicals, and it suits people whose skin reacts to wax or who take medicines that make skin sensitive.

For eyebrows, that precision is the whole point. A thread can take out a single row of hair at a time, which lets us shape slowly and stop before the brow gets too thin.

Threading also works for the upper lip, chin, and sides of the face. It is quick, usually ten to fifteen minutes for brows, and most people are in and out in under half an hour for a full face.

02 How to pick an eyebrow shape that suits your face

Your natural brow shape is your best starting point. We clean it up and define the arch, rather than forcing a shape from a photo that does not fit your bone structure.

A soft arch suits most faces and opens up the eyes without looking harsh. Very high, sharp arches can look severe, and very flat brows can make eyes look heavy, so we aim for balance.

If you have over-plucked brows in the past, tell us. We can map a growth plan, leave certain areas alone for a few weeks, and use a pencil or tint in the meantime so the shape still looks tidy.

03 What happens during your threading appointment

We start by looking at your brows and asking what you want: just a tidy-up, or a new shape. If you are unsure, we will show you the shape with a pencil outline before we start threading.

Threading feels like a quick pinch, repeated in short bursts. Most people get used to it within a minute, and the brow area settles fast. Upper lip threading stings a little more, but it is over in moments.

After threading, we apply a soothing lotion or aloe to calm redness. If your skin is very sensitive, tell us beforehand so we can work more gently and skip any extra products.

04 Threading, waxing, or tweezing: what is the difference

Threading is the most precise of the three and the gentlest on skin, because it touches only the hair and a small area around it. Waxing covers a larger area at once, so it is faster for bigger areas like arms or legs.

Tweezing is best for stray hairs between appointments, not for shaping a whole brow, because it is slow and easy to overdo one hair at a time.

For brows and face, most of our clients stick with threading. For larger areas, waxing may make more sense, and we can talk through both when you visit.

05 Aftercare and how often to come back

Skip makeup on the threaded area for a few hours, and avoid scrubbing or very hot water on the same day. A little redness is normal and usually fades within an hour or two.

Most people need threading every three to four weeks for brows, and every two to three weeks for the upper lip, because facial hair grows back faster there.

Questions we hear at the salon

Does threading hurt?

It feels like a quick pinch, and most people get used to it within a minute. Brows hurt less than the upper lip, and the whole thing is over fast.

How long does redness last after threading?

Usually under an hour for brows, a little longer for sensitive skin. Soothing lotion after threading helps, and makeup should wait a few hours.

Can I get threading if I have acne or sensitive skin?

Yes, threading often suits sensitive skin better than wax. Tell us about acne, medicines, or recent peels so we can work around sore areas.

How often should I get my eyebrows threaded?

Every three to four weeks keeps the shape tidy. Waiting much longer lets stray hairs grow back and makes the next shape harder to keep even.
