Hair Extensions Aftercare: Washing, Sleeping, Styling

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

You got extensions for your best friend's wedding and they looked incredible in every photo. Three weeks later, the tapes are slipping, a small bird's nest is forming at the nape of your neck, and you have been washing them exactly like your own hair. That is the problem. Extensions are not your hair, and treating them like your hair is how they die early. Here is the care routine that keeps them alive.

01 The first week decides everything

Fresh extensions need a settling-in period. For the first 48 to 72 hours, keep washing to a minimum and avoid heavy styling. Do not scratch at the bonds or tapes with your nails. If something itches or pulls, call the salon instead of investigating with your fingers. Most early extension failures, slipping tapes, loosened beads, come from rough handling in week one, not from bad application.

02 Washing without wrecking them

Use a sulphate-free shampoo, always. Sulphates loosen the adhesive on tape-ins and dry out the extension hair, which gets no natural oils from your scalp. Wash with lukewarm water, never hot, and keep your head upright in the shower. Flipping your head upside down tangles sewn and tape-in extensions badly.

Shampoo the scalp and roots where your natural hair is, and let the suds run down the lengths rather than scrubbing the extensions themselves. Condition from mid-length to ends only, never on the tapes, bonds, or beads, because conditioner loosens them. Rinse thoroughly, then gently squeeze out the water. Never wring.

03 Sleeping: where most damage happens

Never sleep with wet extensions. Before bed, make sure they are fully dry, then put your hair in a loose braid or a low, loose ponytail. A silk or satin pillowcase cuts friction dramatically compared to cotton. If you toss and turn, a loose top bun works too, as long as nothing pulls on the attachment points. The nape tangles most people complain about come from sleeping with hair loose on a cotton pillowcase.

04 Styling rules

Heat protectant before every hot tool, no exceptions. Extension hair cannot repair itself, so heat damage on it is permanent. Keep flat irons and curling wands away from the tapes and bonds, because heat melts adhesive. Drop your tool temperature a notch below what you use on your own hair.

Brush with a loop brush or a wide-tooth comb, starting from the ends and working up, holding the roots so you are not pulling on the attachments. Avoid oily or heavy styling products near the roots. Dry shampoo is fine on your natural roots between washes, just keep it away from the bonds. If your scalp feels congested under the extensions, a professional scalp detox clears the buildup safely.

05 The maintenance calendar

Extensions are not fit-and-forget. Tape-ins typically need moving up every six to eight weeks as your hair grows. Sewn-in wefts need tightening on a similar schedule. Clip-ins should be removed every night and never slept in. Book your maintenance appointments when you get the extensions put in, not when they start slipping.

Build a small kit: sulphate-free shampoo, a loop brush, a silk pillowcase, and a microfiber towel. These four things do more for extension lifespan than any expensive serum.

Questions we hear at the salon

How long do hair extensions last?

It depends on the type. Tape-ins and sewn wefts usually last 6 to 8 weeks before they need moving up, and the hair itself can be reused for several months with good care. Clip-ins last a year or more since you remove them daily. Lifespan comes down to aftercare more than anything else.

Can I oil my hair with extensions?

Light oiling on the lengths is fine, but keep oil away from the tapes, bonds, and beads, since oil loosens the adhesive. Apply oil from mid-length to ends only, and wash it out thoroughly. Heavy oiling near the roots is the fastest way to make tape-ins slip.

Can I swim with hair extensions?

You can, but both chlorinated and salt water are rough on extensions. Wet your hair with clean water first, put it in a braid, and rinse immediately after swimming. A swim cap is the safest option. Expect the extensions' lifespan to shorten if you swim regularly.

Why are my extensions tangling at the nape?

Nape tangling usually comes from friction: sleeping with hair loose, high collars, or not drying fully before bed. A loose braid at night and a silk pillowcase fix most cases. If matting keeps returning despite good care, the extensions may need repositioning at the salon.
