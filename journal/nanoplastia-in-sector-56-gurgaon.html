If you have heard of keratin smoothening and wished it were gentler, nanoplastia is probably what you were looking for. It is a smoothing treatment built on amino acids and proteins instead of harsh chemicals. No strong fumes, no burning eyes, and hair that comes out smooth, manageable, and glossy. At The Stylist, nanoplastia has become the treatment we recommend most for people who want frizz gone without the intensity of keratin or rebonding.

01 What nanoplastia actually is

Nanoplastia uses amino acids, proteins, and fruit acids that penetrate the hair shaft and rebuild it from the inside. The particles are small enough to pass the cuticle, which is where the nano in the name comes from. Heat from a flat iron seals them in, and the hair dries smoother and shinier.

The key difference from keratin is the chemistry. Many keratin treatments rely on formaldehyde or chemicals that release it when heated, which is why salons smell sharp during the process. Nanoplastia is formaldehyde-free, so the appointment is comfortable, and it suits people who found keratin too strong.

02 Nanoplastia vs keratin: the honest comparison

Both smooth frizz and cut styling time. Keratin is stronger on very coarse, resistant hair and gives a flatter finish. Nanoplastia gives a more natural finish with movement and better condition, because it treats while it smooths. For fine, wavy, or coloured hair, nanoplastia is the safer pick.

Longevity is similar: three to five months with proper aftercare, and the price sits in the same range. The deciding factor is your hair history. Tell us what your hair has been through and we will point you at the right one, not the expensive one.

03 Who nanoplastia suits best

It suits wavy to curly hair that frizzes in humidity, colour-treated hair that keratin would stress, fine hair that needs smoothing without flattening, and anyone who wants easier mornings. If your hair is extremely coarse and you want it pin-straight, keratin or rebonding may be the honest recommendation.

It does not suit severely damaged or breaking hair. Smoothing treatments need reasonably intact hair to work on. If your hair snaps when stretched, we will suggest repair first and revisit smoothing once it is stronger. We would rather tell you now than watch a treatment fail.

04 What the appointment looks like

Plan for two and a half to three hours. We wash with a clarifying shampoo, apply the formula section by section, let it process, rinse, blow-dry, and seal it in with a flat iron. You will see the difference before you leave the chair: hair that falls smooth and catches the light.

Washing at home is uneventful, which is the point. Hair dries smoother, needs less heat styling, and holds its shape in humidity. Most people drop to a wash and a quick dry in the morning, and the hair still looks done.

05 Aftercare that keeps it going

Use a sulphate-free shampoo and conditioner. Sleep on dry hair, not damp. Avoid chlorine for the first couple of weeks, and if you swim, wet your hair with clean water and conditioner before getting in. These small habits separate a three-month result from a five-month one.

Skip heavy oiling before wash days; light serums suit smoothed hair better. If frizz returns at the roots first, come back for a root touch-up without redoing the full treatment. The shine test: if your hair looks dull even right after a fresh wash, the cuticle is rough and smoothing will change how light bounces off it. That is the clearest sign nanoplastia will suit you.

Questions we hear at the salon

Is nanoplastia better than keratin?

For fine, wavy, or colour-treated hair, usually yes. Nanoplastia is gentler, formaldehyde-free, and leaves natural movement with great shine. Keratin is stronger on very coarse hair. Your hair history decides, not the price tag.

How long does nanoplastia last?

Three to five months with sulphate-free aftercare. Avoiding chlorine and harsh shampoo stretches the result. Roots may need a touch-up before the lengths do.

Does nanoplastia damage hair?

It is one of the gentlest smoothing options. It rebuilds with proteins and amino acids instead of harsh chemicals, so hair usually feels healthier after. It does not suit severely broken hair, which needs repair first.
