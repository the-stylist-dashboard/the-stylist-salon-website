01 What highlights really do

Highlights are thin sections of hair lightened to add depth and movement. Done well, they make hair look fuller and more interesting without changing your overall colour completely. Done badly, they look like stripes. The difference comes down to placement, shade choice, and how much hair is taken in each section.

For most naturally dark Indian hair, the best highlights are two to three shades lighter than your base, not blonde straight away. Soft caramel, honey, and warm brown tones blend naturally and grow out gracefully.

If this is your first time, bring photos of looks you like and ones you do not. Both help your stylist understand what you mean by natural.

02 Types of highlights worth knowing

Classic foil highlights give the most control. Your stylist paints selected strands and wraps them in foil, so the colour lifts evenly and the result is neat and predictable. This suits people who want a polished, even finish.

Balayage is painted freehand, with more colour towards the ends and a softer blend at the roots. It grows out with almost no harsh line, so it is the lowest maintenance option and a good pick if you do not want salon visits every six weeks.

Face-framing highlights, sometimes called money-piece highlights, lighten just the strands around your face. They brighten your features without lightening your whole head, and they cost less than a full head of highlights.

Ask your stylist which method suits your hair length and budget.

03 What to expect at your appointment

Your visit starts with a consultation. We check your natural colour, any previous colour or keratin work, and the condition of your hair. Hair that has been coloured recently or treated with smoothening products lifts differently, and your stylist needs to know that before mixing colour.

Colouring takes one to two hours depending on how much hair is highlighted. After that comes toning, which decides whether your highlights look warm and rich or brassy and orange. Never skip the toner.

You will leave with a wash and blow dry so you can see the real result in daylight. If the tone looks off, say so there and then. Small adjustments are easy on the day and much harder later.

04 How to look after highlights at home

Highlighted hair needs a bit more care than untouched hair. Use a shampoo for coloured hair, wash with lukewarm water, and keep heat styling moderate. A weekly mask keeps lightened sections soft instead of dry.

Purple shampoo helps only if your highlights are cool-toned. On warm caramel or honey highlights it can dull the colour, so check with your stylist first.

Plan a toner refresh every six to eight weeks if you want the colour to stay fresh. The highlights themselves can last three to four months before they need redoing, especially with balayage.

Questions we hear at the salon

Do highlights damage your hair?

Lightening does stress the hair, but careful application, the right developer strength, and a good mask afterwards keep damage minimal. Very damaged hair may need a conditioning treatment first.

How long do highlights last?

The colour stays until it grows out or is recoloured. Balayage can look good for three to four months, while classic foils need a touch-up every six to eight weeks as roots show.

Can I get highlights on coloured hair?

Usually yes, but previous colour changes how the new colour lifts. Tell your stylist about every colour, keratin, or smoothening treatment you have had so they can plan safely.

What is the difference between highlights and balayage?

Highlights are usually done with foils for an even, defined result. Balayage is painted freehand for a softer, sun-kissed blend with less obvious regrowth.
