Hair Colour for Men: Covering Grey Without Looking Dyed

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

It starts at the temples. A few silver strands you pretend not to see in the bathroom mirror, then the sideburns join in, and one day a colleague asks if you are "going for the distinguished look." You are not. You just want the grey gone without walking around looking like you dipped your head in shoe polish. That is a solvable problem, but only if the colour is chosen with restraint.

01 Why men's grey coverage goes wrong

Bad men's colour has a look, and you know it: flat jet black from hairline to nape, skin stained at the edges, zero variation. It happens for two reasons. The shade is darker than the man's natural hair ever was, and the colour goes on everywhere when only the temples needed it.

Natural hair is never one flat tone. It has depth, lighter and darker strands mixed together. Good grey coverage imitates that variation instead of painting over it. The goal is for people to notice you look rested, not to notice your hair.

02 Shade matching: match your real dark

Match your natural dark, not your idea of dark. Most Indian men do best with a dark brown or a soft black. Jet black looks painted against real skin and it photographs terribly, that flat helmet-head effect in every picture.

When in doubt, go one level lighter than you think. A slightly lighter shade reads as natural hair with dimension. A shade too dark reads as dye from across the room, and there is no talking your way out of that once it is on.

03 Partial coverage beats full coverage, early on

Grey arrives in zones: temples first, then sideburns, then the beard. If the top of your head is still dark, colouring all of it is overkill that creates the flat look. Targeted coverage on the greying zones blends everything without touching hair that does not need it.

Camouflage colouring deserves a mention here. It is a gentler, shorter-lasting option that blends grey rather than covering it completely, and it fades gradually instead of leaving a hard regrowth line. For salt-and-pepper hair, where you want to tone down the grey without erasing it, it is often the smartest first step. 04 What the colourist does in the chair

A proper service starts with a consultation, not a bowl of colour. The colourist studies your natural shade, checks how much grey there is and where, and picks the formula. A strand test is standard practice before the first application, to check both the shade and how your skin reacts.

Application takes twenty to forty-five minutes depending on coverage, then processing time while the colour develops. A good colourist also colours the beard to match if it is going grey at a different rate than the hair, because mismatched head and beard is the detail that gives the game away.

05 The maintenance reality

Here is the honest part. Permanent colour lasts until it grows out, so the regrowth at the roots shows in three to four weeks. That is the schedule you are signing up for. The upside for men: if you cut your hair monthly anyway, the colour gets refreshed with the haircut and the whole thing becomes one appointment.

Between visits, a sulfate-free shampoo and not washing daily keep the colour from fading fast. 06 The downsides, stated plainly

Colour is a commitment. Miss the maintenance window and the regrowth line announces itself. Over a year, the cost adds up, so factor that in before the first tube is opened. And if you ever want to stop, growing it out means a few awkward months of two-tone hair unless your barber blends the transition with the right cuts.

Questions we hear at the salon

How do I pick a shade that looks natural?

Match your natural dark, not your idea of dark. Most Indian men do best with dark brown or soft black rather than jet black, which looks painted against real skin. Bring a photo of yourself from five years ago so the colourist sees your actual target shade. When in doubt, go one level lighter.

How long does men's grey coverage last?

Permanent colour lasts until it grows out, so the visible regrowth at the roots usually shows in three to four weeks. Camouflage colour fades gradually over a few weeks and the regrowth line is softer. Your haircut cycle decides the real schedule: if you cut every month, the colour gets refreshed with it.

Can I colour just my temples and beard instead of all my hair?

Yes, and for early greying it is often the better choice. Temples, sideburns and the beard go grey first, so targeted coverage there blends everything without touching hair that does not need it. It costs less, takes less time, and looks more natural than a full-head application done too early.

Does colouring damage men's hair?

Any permanent colour stresses the hair to some degree, though on short men's hair the effect is limited because the coloured lengths get cut off regularly. Camouflage and demi-permanent options are gentler. Using a sulfate-free shampoo and not washing daily keeps the colour and the hair in better shape between appointments.
