Hair botox sounds like a salon buzzword, but it is a real treatment with a specific job: repairing hair that has been damaged by colour, bleach, heat, or years of rough handling. If your hair feels rough, tangles easily, and never looks smooth no matter what you put on it, this is worth understanding. Here is what it does, who it suits, and what to expect near Sector 47.

01 What hair botox actually is

Despite the name, hair botox has nothing to do with injections. It is a deep conditioning treatment packed with proteins, amino acids, and oils that fill in the gaps in damaged hair. Damaged hair is porous, which means the outer layer has tiny cracks that let moisture escape and frizz in. The treatment fills those cracks, so the hair feels smoother, thicker, and easier to manage. It is not a straightening treatment, though repaired hair often looks straighter simply because it lies flat. Think of it as a repair job, not a style change.

02 Who it helps most

Hair botox helps most when the damage is real. That means hair that has been bleached, coloured repeatedly, or flat-ironed most days. It also helps hair that feels stretchy when wet or breaks when you brush it. If your hair is healthy and just a bit dry, a regular hair spa does the same job for less money. Botox is also a good pick before a big event if your hair needs to look polished for weeks, not just for one evening.

03 What the appointment looks like

Expect 90 minutes to two hours. The stylist washes your hair, applies the botox treatment section by section, and leaves it to soak in so the hair can absorb it. Then the hair is blow-dried and sealed with a flat iron, which locks the treatment into the hair shaft. That sealing step is what makes the result last longer than a normal mask. Tell the stylist about any colour or chemical work in the last six months, because that changes how the treatment is applied.

04 How long it lasts, and how to keep it

Results usually last four to eight weeks, depending on how often you wash your hair and how damaged it was to begin with. To stretch the result, use a sulphate-free shampoo, wash in lukewarm water rather than hot, and skip daily heat styling where you can. A follow-up hair spa two or three weeks later helps maintain the smoothness without overloading the hair. When the frizz starts creeping back, that is your cue to book again rather than waiting until the damage returns fully.

05 Botox, keratin, and hair spa: which one

These three get mixed up constantly. A hair spa is maintenance: hydration and shine for a week or two. Hair botox is repair: it fills damage and lasts four to eight weeks. Keratin is smoothing and straightening, stronger and longer lasting, but also a bigger commitment and not right for everyone. If your main complaint is frizz and your hair is damaged, botox is the middle path. If you want pin-straight hair for months, ask about keratin instead. Questions we hear at the salon

Does hair botox straighten hair?

Not exactly. It repairs damage, and repaired hair lies flatter, so it can look straighter. But it is not a straightening treatment like keratin or rebonding. If you want permanently straight hair, ask about keratin instead.

Is hair botox safe for coloured hair?

Yes, it is often best for coloured hair, since colour is a common cause of the damage it repairs. Tell your stylist when you last coloured your hair so they can adjust the treatment and avoid over-processing.

How soon can I wash my hair after botox?

Most stylists suggest waiting 24 to 48 hours before the first wash, so the treatment can settle fully. After that, use a sulphate-free shampoo to make the result last as long as possible.
