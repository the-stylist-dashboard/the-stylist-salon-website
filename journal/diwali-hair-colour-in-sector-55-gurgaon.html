01 Which colour suits Diwali

Diwali hair colour is a global colour, highlights, or balayage done in time for Diwali on Sunday 8 November, so the colour looks rich and settled, not freshly done. It suits anyone who wants a visible change or a refresh before the festival. A global colour takes about 90 to 120 minutes, highlights take about 120 to 150 minutes, and balayage takes about 150 to 180 minutes.

For Diwali, warm tones photograph beautifully: chocolate brown, caramel highlights, warm copper, and soft balayage that catches evening light. Cool ash tones suit some skin tones too, but they need more upkeep and can look dull under warm festival lighting if the base is not right.

Bring photos of colours you like, and tell us your skin tone and how much upkeep you will do. A colour that needs a salon visit every three weeks is not the right pick if you visit every two months. Honest advice now saves a faded colour later.

02 When to book for Diwali

Book colour at least a week before Diwali, ideally in the last week of October or the first days of November. Fresh colour needs a few washes to settle, and a week gives you time to adjust if the tone needs a tweak. Booking on Diwali week itself leaves no room for that.

Diwali week chairs fill fast, especially for colour, because colour takes the longest chair time. Call 81781 59945 now, even if your visit is next week, so the slot is held. Waiting till Diwali week often means no colour slot at all.

03 Colour, highlights, or balayage

Global colour changes the whole head to one shade, and suits a full refresh or covering greys. Highlights add lighter strands through the hair, and suit a softer change with easier grow-out. Balayage is hand-painted colour that grows out most naturally, and suits busy people who cannot visit often.

If you have never coloured before, highlights or balayage are gentler first steps than a full global colour. If you colour regularly, a root touch-up plus a gloss refreshes the whole look in less time. Tell us your colour history, because past colour changes what we can do safely.

04 Aftercare that keeps colour rich

Use a sulphate-free shampoo, wash with lukewarm water, and skip very hot showers for the first week. Heat styling without protection fades colour faster than anything else, so use a heat protectant if you style with tools. A hair spa a week or two after colour also helps, because coloured hair needs moisture.

Chlorine and hard water fade colour too, so rinse hair after swimming and use a shower filter if your water is very hard. Small habits like these stretch a colour by weeks, which matters when the next salon visit is after Diwali.
