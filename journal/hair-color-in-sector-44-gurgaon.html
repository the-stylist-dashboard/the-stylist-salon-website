Hair colour looks simple from the outside: pick a shade, sit in the chair, walk out transformed. The reality is messier. The wrong shade can wash you out, the wrong technique can fry your ends, and skipping a patch test can land you in real trouble. 01 Global colour vs highlights: pick your technique

Global colour means one shade applied from roots to ends, all over. Highlights mean selected strands are lightened to add dimension. They solve different problems and ask for different commitments:

Best forCovering greys, a full new look, rich dark shadesAdding depth and brightness without changing the base

If you want grey coverage, global colour is usually the answer. If you want sun-kissed dimension that grows out gracefully, highlights or balayage suit you better. Tell your stylist what you want to see in the mirror, and they will point you to the right technique. 02 Choosing shades for Indian skin tones

Indian skin has warm undertones, and the shades that flatter it follow that warmth. Rich chocolate browns, warm caramel, copper, and auburn sit beautifully against warm skin. Deep, glossy blacks and espresso shades look expensive and natural. What tends to go wrong: cool ash tones and very pale blondes, which can wash out warm complexions and look disconnected from the face.

A good rule is to stay within two to three levels of your natural colour for your first colour service. That keeps the result flattering and easy to maintain. If you are dreaming of something bolder, ask your stylist to show you the shade on a swatch against your face in daylight. Phone screens lie. 03 The patch test is not optional

Colour allergies are rare but real, and they can be serious. That is why we do a patch test 48 hours before your first colour service with us. A small amount of product goes behind your ear or on your inner arm, and if your skin reacts, we know before it matters.

Be honest with us about your history: any itching, rashes, or swelling from hair dye in the past. Also tell us if you have henna or mehndi on your hair, because henna changes how colour develops and mixing the two can give patchy or greenish results. A strand test sorts out what is possible. Any salon that skips the patch test for a first-time colour client is cutting a corner you should not let them cut.

04 Aftercare that stops the colour from fading

The colour leaves the salon looking perfect. Whether it stays that way is on you. Four habits protect it:

  • Use a sulphate-free shampoo. Regular shampoos strip colour fast. A colour-safe shampoo is the single biggest thing you can do.
  • Wash in lukewarm water. Hot water opens the hair cuticle and lets colour bleed out. Keep it cool, especially on wash day.
  • Use heat protectant. Straighteners and curlers bake colour out. Spray first, style after.
  • Cover up in the sun. Gurgaon sun fades colour the way it fades car dashboards. A cap or scarf on long outdoor days helps more than you would think.

Also, skip anti-dandruff shampoos unless you actually have dandruff. They are strong by design and will eat through fresh colour quickly.

The henna conversation: if you have used henna in the last year, tell your stylist before anything else. Pure henna coats the hair and resists chemical colour, and some compound hennas contain metallic salts that react badly with dye. This is the one thing clients forget to mention, and it matters the most.

Questions we hear at the salon

Will hair colour damage my hair?

Colour does change the hair's structure, so some dryness is normal, but damage comes from over-processing, not from colour itself. Choosing the right shade, using proper developer strength, and following with good aftercare keeps the hair healthy. Bleach-heavy colours need more care than deposit-only shades.

How long does hair colour last?

Permanent colour grows out at the roots in 4 to 6 weeks and needs touch-ups to stay neat. The colour on the lengths can stay rich for months with sulphate-free shampoo and proper care. Highlights and balayage grow out more softly, so they need fewer salon visits.

I have henna on my hair. Can I still get colour?

Tell your stylist first, because henna changes how colour develops. Pure henna usually blocks or alters chemical colour, and mixing the two can give uneven or greenish results. A strand test tells us what is possible, so never skip that conversation.

Do you do a patch test before colouring?

Yes, we do a patch test 48 hours before your first colour service with us, especially for first-time clients and anyone with sensitive skin. It takes a minute to apply and protects you from a serious allergic reaction. No good salon skips this step.
