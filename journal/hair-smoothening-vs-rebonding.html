Hair Smoothening vs Rebonding: Which One Is Which

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

You walk into the salon saying you want smoothening, and the stylist asks whether you mean smoothening or rebonding. You stare back, because you thought they were the same thing. They are not, and picking the wrong one is how people end up with hair flatter than they wanted, or straighter than they can maintain. Here is the difference, plainly.

01 Why everyone mixes them up

Both are chemical straightening treatments done in a salon, both take hours, and both leave you walking out looking sleek. Salons sometimes use the words loosely, which does not help. But the formulas differ, the results differ, and the regrowth experience is completely different. Knowing which is which before you book saves you from months of regret.

02 Rebonding: the permanent straight

Rebonding breaks down your hair's natural texture and rebuilds it straight. The result is pin-straight hair that stays straight through washes, humidity, and sleep. It does not wash out. It grows out, which means your roots come in with your natural texture while the lengths stay straight.

The appointment is long, often 5 to 6 hours on thick Indian hair. The aftercare is strict: no washing or tying for 72 hours, sulphate-free products after. If pin-straight is exactly what you want and you can commit to the upkeep, rebonding delivers. We cover the full experience in our rebonding guide for Indian hair.

03 Smoothening: the softer option

Smoothening uses a milder formula to relax frizz and waves into a soft, natural-looking straight. The finish has movement. It is not pin-straight, and it is not meant to be. Results last 2 to 4 months and fade gradually, so the regrowth is far less dramatic than rebonding's line of demarcation.

The appointment runs 3 to 4 hours, and the aftercare is similar but slightly more forgiving. If you like your hair's body but hate the frizz and the daily straightening battle, smoothening is usually the better call.

05 How to choose

Ask yourself three questions. Do I want my hair to look naturally straight or ruler straight? Can I handle a visible regrowth line every few months? Am I willing to sit through a six-hour appointment and strict aftercare? If your answers are natural, no, and maybe, smoothening is your treatment. If you want permanent straight and accept the maintenance, rebonding.

Questions we hear at the salon

Is smoothening less damaging than rebonding?

Generally yes, smoothening uses a milder formula and the results are temporary, so the chemical load on your hair is lower. But both are chemical treatments with heat involved, so neither is damage-free. Good aftercare and spacing out chemical services matter more than which of the two you pick.

Can I colour my hair after smoothening or rebonding?

Wait at least two to three weeks after the straightening treatment before colouring, and tell your colourist what was done. Doing both too close together stresses the hair. A strand test before colour is wise, especially on rebonded hair, which has already been through a strong chemical process.

How often can I repeat smoothening?

Most people repeat smoothening every 3 to 4 months as the effect fades. Rebonding is usually only redone on new growth, since the treated lengths stay straight permanently. Repeating either too often on the same lengths increases dryness and breakage, so your stylist should assess the hair first.

Will smoothening work on very curly hair?

It will relax the curl and cut frizz, but very tight curls will not go fully straight with smoothening. If you have tight curls and want them straight, rebonding or nanoplastia are more effective. Be honest about your goal at the consultation so the stylist picks the right treatment.
