Hair Colour for Men: Grey Coverage That Looks Natural

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 27 September 2026 · 6 min read

Most men colour their hair one of two ways: box dye at home that goes too dark, or nothing at all while the grey multiplies. There is a middle path that salons do well and boxes do badly: colour matched to your skin and natural base, applied so it looks like your hair, just younger. Here is how to get it right.

The short answer

The most natural-looking option for men is grey blending, which softens grey into a salt-and-pepper effect instead of covering it completely, growing out without a visible line. Full coverage suits men who want zero visible grey but needs root touch-ups every 3 to 4 weeks. Either way, stay within one or two levels of your natural colour, and colour the beard to match the hair.

01 Why men's colour usually goes wrong

Too dark. Box dyes are one-shade-fits-all, which on most Indian men means jet black over 40 percent grey. The result reads as helmet hair from across the room.

Stained skin. Home application leaves colour on the forehead, ears and neck. A salon's barrier cream and cleanup exist precisely for this.

The beard mismatch. Coloured hair with a fully grey beard, or vice versa, breaks the illusion instantly. Hair and beard have to be planned as a pair, because they are seen as one.

Blending is the safer first move. It is subtle enough that colleagues notice you look rested rather than coloured, and stepping up to fuller coverage later is easy. Going straight to full coverage and regretting the severity is the more common mistake.

03 Choosing the shade

Stay within one or two levels of your natural colour. For most Indian men that means dark browns, not jet black, unless your natural base was truly black. The goal is the colour your hair was five years ago, not a new identity.

Undertone matters more than brand. Your stylist should assess your shade in natural daylight against your skin, not under salon spots that flatter everything. If the consultation happens entirely under warm indoor light, ask to step toward the door.

04 The beard has to match

Beard hair is coarser and more porous than scalp hair, so it grabs colour darker and faster. Applied identically, the beard ends up a shade deeper than the head, which looks wrong.

We mix and time them as a pair in the same appointment: usually a slightly softer formula on the beard, processed for less time, checked against the hair before rinsing. Ten extra minutes of attention here is what separates natural from obvious.

05 Upkeep in Gurugram

Colour-safe, sulphate-free shampoo; washing less often; and accepting that Gurugram sun fades colour faster than winter light does. A cap in peak afternoon sun is not vanity, it is colour insurance.

Touch-up rhythm: every 3 to 4 weeks for full coverage, 5 to 6 for blending. Blending stretches further because there is no regrowth line announcing the delay.

The daylight test. Check the colour in natural light before you leave the salon. Indoor lighting flatters every shade; daylight tells the truth about whether it matches you.

Hair Colour for Men questions, answered

How long does men's hair colour last?

Grey blending fades gradually over 3 to 4 weeks. Permanent full coverage is permanent on the hair itself, but regrowth shows in 3 to 4 weeks and needs touch-ups.

Will hair colour damage my hair?

Demi-permanent blending is very gentle. Permanent colour causes some dryness, manageable with conditioning. The bigger risk is overlapping permanent colour onto already-coloured hair; a salon avoids this by applying only to the regrowth.

Can I colour only my beard?

Yes, beard-only colour is a common standalone service. It takes 10 to 15 minutes and is the fastest visible refresh for men whose grey starts at the chin.

Does hair colour cover white hair fully?

Permanent colour does, in one sitting. Grey blending deliberately does not; it leaves a natural salt-and-pepper effect that many men prefer because it grows out invisibly.
