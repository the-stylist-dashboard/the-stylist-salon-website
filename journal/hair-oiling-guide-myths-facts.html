Hair Oiling: Myths, Facts and What Actually Helps

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

Your grandmother swears by overnight coconut oil. Your favourite influencer says oiling causes breakouts on the scalp. Your hairdresser told you to stop oiling before your colour appointment. Everyone has a strong opinion about hair oil, and half of them contradict each other. After years of watching what oiling actually does on the salon floor, here is the honest split between myth and fact.

01 What oil actually does

Let us start with the unglamorous truth. Oil does not feed the hair; the shaft is not living tissue and absorbs very little. What oil does well is coat. A light coating reduces friction between strands, which means less tangling, less breakage while detangling, and smoother-feeling hair after the wash. On the scalp, a massage with oil feels good and loosens dry surface flakes before a wash, which is genuinely useful.

That is the real, observable benefit: lubrication. Everything beyond that is marketing or nostalgia. And lubrication is not nothing. For dry, tangle-prone hair, it is the difference between a painless detangle and a handful of snapped strands.

02 Myths worth retiring

  • "Overnight oiling fixes everything." Longer is not better. Thirty to sixty minutes before a wash gives you the full benefit; overnight mostly gives you stained pillowcases and oil that is harder to wash out.
  • "Oiling grows hair / stops hair fall." Oil cannot change the growth cycle or regrow hair. It reduces breakage from friction, which means you keep more of the length you grow. That is a real benefit, but it is not growth.
  • "More oil means more benefit." A heavy soak needs harsh washing to remove, and that harsh wash undoes the good. A light, even coating wins.
  • "Everyone should oil weekly." Fine, low-porosity hair can look limp and weighed down with regular oiling. If your hair hates it, you are not doing it wrong; it is just not for your hair type.

03 The method that works

Warm a small amount of oil between your palms. Coconut oil suits most Indian hair; almond and sesame are lighter alternatives if coconut feels heavy. Work it through the lengths and ends, where friction damage happens, with only a light touch on the scalp. Leave it thirty to sixty minutes. Then shampoo twice: the first wash moves the oil, the second cleans the scalp. If your hair still feels coated after two washes, you used too much.

Time it before your regular wash day, not as a separate event. And skip oiling right before colour or smoothing treatments; most stylists prefer working on clean, product-free hair, so the treatment takes evenly.

The two-wash rule: if oil is still in your hair after shampooing twice, the amount was the problem, not the shampoo. Use less next time rather than a harsher shampoo this time.

04 When to skip the oil

Skip oiling on a flaky, itchy scalp; oil can trap flakes and make things look worse. Skip it before a salon chemical service unless your stylist asks for it. And in Gurugram's hard water, be aware that oil plus mineral film can leave hair feeling coated even after washing, so keep the application light and the clarifying wash in your routine. Questions we hear at the salon

How long should I leave oil in my hair?

Thirty to sixty minutes before washing gives the full benefit. Oil works by coating the hair and reducing friction, and that happens quickly. Overnight oiling adds little beyond stained pillowcases and oil that is harder to wash out. Time it before your regular wash day rather than making it a separate event.

Does oiling make hair grow faster?

No. Oil cannot change the hair growth cycle or regrow hair. What it does is reduce breakage from friction and tangling, so you keep more of the length you grow. That is a genuine benefit for dry, tangle-prone hair, but it is breakage prevention, not growth.

Which oil is best for hair?

Coconut oil suits most Indian hair types and is the traditional default for good reason. Almond and sesame oils are lighter alternatives if coconut feels heavy or weighs fine hair down. The application matters more than the choice: a light, even coating on the lengths beats a heavy soak of any oil.

Should I oil my scalp if it is flaky or itchy?

Better not. Oil can trap dead skin against a flaky scalp and make things look worse. If the scalp is persistently itchy, sore or red, see a dermatologist rather than oiling through it. A light oil massage is fine on a comfortable, healthy scalp, as long as you shampoo it out fully.
