Eyebrow Threading: Shapes That Suit Your Face

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

"Just clean them up" is the most dangerous sentence in eyebrow threading. It means something different to every threader, and it is how good brows become thin, surprised-looking arches. Brows frame your whole face, and the right shape depends on your bone structure, not on whatever is trending this season. Here is how to walk in knowing exactly what to ask for.

01 Mapping shape to face

Oval faces suit a soft, balanced arch with medium thickness. Round faces benefit from a slightly higher arch, which adds the illusion of length. Square faces look best with a strong, defined brow that balances a strong jawline. Heart-shaped faces suit a rounded, softer arch that does not add width at the forehead. Long faces do well with flatter, more horizontal brows that visually shorten the face. These are starting points, not rules; your natural brow bone always has the final say.

02 What to tell your threader

Bring a photo of brows you like on a face shaped like yours. Then say three things: thickness (keep it thick, medium, or thin), arch (soft, defined, or high), and length (where the tail should end). Ask the threader to show you the shape before removing more. A good threader steps back, checks symmetry from a distance, and removes a little at a time. If someone starts threading without asking a single question, speak up or stop the session.

03 Fixing over-plucked brows

Thin, tadpole-shaped brows from years of over-threading need patience, not product. Stop threading the top line entirely and let the hair grow for six to eight weeks. Book clean-ups where the threader only removes strays below the brow and between the brows. Growth is slow and uneven, which is normal. Resist the urge to "even them out" every week; that habit is how they got thin in the first place.

A brow pencil or powder fills the gaps meanwhile, which makes the grow-out phase far less painful to live with.

04 Aftercare and upkeep

Expect mild redness for an hour or so; a cold compress or plain aloe helps. Avoid touching the area with unwashed hands and skip heavy creams on the brows for the rest of the day. Most clients rebook every three to four weeks, though finer hair can stretch longer. If one brow grows faster than the other, the threader can map an ideal shape and you only tidy the strays between visits. Men often add a brow clean-up to a haircut visit since it takes five extra minutes. And if you colour your hair, plan brows and colour together; our balayage vs highlights guide covers face-framing shades that work with your features.

Questions we hear at the salon

How often should I thread my eyebrows?

Most clients come back every three to four weeks, when regrowth starts softening the shape. Very fine hair can stretch longer. Avoid weekly touch-ups; constant re-threading is what thins brows out over time. Let the shape grow in a little between visits.

Threading or waxing for eyebrows?

Threading gives more precise control, which is why most brow specialists prefer it. Waxing is faster but removes a wider strip of skin along with the hair and can irritate the delicate eye area. For detailed shaping, threading wins every time.

Can threading fix uneven brows?

It can improve them a lot, but not instantly. The threader maps both brows to your bone structure and removes strategically over a few sessions. Badly uneven brows may need a grow-out period first; patience beats trying to fix everything in one sitting.

Does eyebrow threading hurt a lot?

It stings, but each pull lasts a second and the whole clean-up takes minutes. Most people find it very manageable, and it gets easier as you get used to the sensation. The area above the brow hurts less than the sensitive skin near the inner corners.
