01 What a wedding hair trial is

A wedding hair trial is a full practice run of your wedding hairstyle, done one to two weeks before the main ceremony, with the same stylist who will do it on the day. The stylist builds the style on your hair, checks how it holds, and takes photos from every angle, so the wedding day styling can follow the same plan. The trial also covers the pins, the jewellery, and the dupatta or veil, because the hair has to sit well with all of them.

The trial is also the time to say what you do not like, because a stylist cannot fix what they do not know about. If the bun feels heavy, if the pins pull, or if the style looks too flat from the side, say so plainly. A good trial is a conversation, not a silent sitting, and the stylist should ask how the style feels, not just how it looks.

02 When to book the trial

Book the trial one to two weeks before the wedding, after your cut and colour are done, so the trial shows the true final hair. If you book the trial too early, before the cut or the colour, the result will not match the wedding day hair. Book the trial itself a few weeks ahead, because wedding season trial slots fill fast in Gurugram, and a rushed trial the day before the wedding leaves no time to fix anything.

Tell the salon your function dates when you book, so the trial can be placed with enough gap for dressing and travel on the wedding day. If you have more than one function with different looks, book one trial for the main ceremony look and discuss the other looks in the same visit, so the whole week is planned together.

03 What to bring to the trial

Bring photos of your outfits for each function, a photo of your jewellery, and the hair accessories you plan to wear, because the trial needs the real pieces to look right. Wear a top with a neckline like your wedding outfit if you can, so the style can be judged against the right shape. Also bring the veil or dupatta if you plan to wear one, because it changes how the hair must sit.

Bring photos of hairstyles you like, and photos of styles you do not like, because both help the stylist understand your taste. Tell the stylist how long each function lasts and how much dancing you plan to do, because a style that survives a long ceremony and a sangeet needs to be secure. The more the stylist knows, the better the trial will be.
