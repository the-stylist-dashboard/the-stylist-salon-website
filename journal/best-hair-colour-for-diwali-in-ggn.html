01 Pick a colour for your skin tone and outfit

Warm skin tones usually suit warm browns, copper, caramel, and golden shades, while cool skin tones usually suit ash browns, cool black, and muted burgundy. Bring a photo of your Diwali outfit, because the colour should sit well with the outfit shade, not fight it. A deep red lehenga and a bright copper colour can clash, while a softer brown keeps the focus on you.

If you are unsure, ask for a shade that is one or two tones away from your natural colour, not a drastic change. A gentle change looks polished and grows out gracefully, while a very light shade on dark hair needs more upkeep and more visits. Your stylist can show you how a shade will look in daylight, not just under salon lights.

02 Global colour, highlights, or balayage for Diwali

Global colour suits a full change, giving one even shade from roots to ends, and it suits clients who want a clear, bold Diwali look. Highlights suit a softer change, adding lighter strands that catch the light in photos, and they grow out more gently than a full colour. Balayage suits a natural, sun-kissed finish with soft, blended ends and very little harsh regrowth line.

Pick by upkeep as much as by look. Global colour needs root touch-ups sooner, while highlights and balayage can go longer between visits. If you have a wedding season after Diwali, a softer option may suit better, because it keeps your options open for later functions. Tell your stylist about your plans after Diwali, so the colour is planned for the whole season.

03 Timing your Diwali colour

Book your Diwali colour three to five days before your main function, so the shade settles and any mild dryness can be treated. Colour done on the day of a function leaves no time for a correction if the shade is not right. A gap of a few days also lets you wash once and see how the colour truly looks.

Avoid a major colour change in the final two days before Diwali, especially if you have never coloured your hair before. A first colour needs a patch test and a consultation, and both need time. If your hair is dry or damaged, a hair spa a week before the colour helps the shade take evenly and last longer.
