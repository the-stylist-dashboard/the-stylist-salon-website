01 What works for a mehndi night

A mehndi night is a long evening, so the hairstyle should be comfortable and easy to wear for hours. Heavy, stiff styles do not suit the mood, and they come loose the moment the dancing starts. Soft braids, loose buns, and half-up styles hold well and still look pretty in photographs, and they leave your face open while the henna is applied to your hands.

Fresh flowers suit mehndi night hair better than heavy jewellery, and a small gajra or a few marigold buds tucked into a braid adds colour without weighing the style down. Keep the front of the hair away from the face with a light pin or a soft twist, so the hair stays out of the way while you sit with your hands out for the henna.

02 Styles that survive the dancing

A side braid is the most reliable mehndi night style, because it stays in place, shows off the flowers, and survives dancing and hugs without falling apart. A low bun works well too, especially if your outfit has a heavy dupatta, because the bun keeps the hair clear of the fabric and the neck. Both styles take less time than a full updo, and they can be finished with a light spray so the hair holds without feeling hard.

Soft waves are a good choice if you want your hair open, but they need to be set well, because the heat and the dancing can drop the curl. Ask for waves that are pinned or braided loosely at the sides, so the front stays tidy even as the curls relax. Open hair also picks up henna more easily, so keep the hair tied back while the henna is being applied.

03 Flowers, gajra, and hair pieces

Most mehndi night outfits are green, yellow, or orange, so the flowers should pick up one of those colours. A marigold gajra suits a green outfit, while white jasmine suits a softer pastel look, and both are light enough to wear for hours. Tell the stylist your outfit colour at booking, so the flowers and the finish can be planned to match instead of clashing with it.

If your outfit has a dupatta that needs pinning to the hair, tell the stylist before the style is set, because the pins need to go in at the right place and the style needs to hold their weight. A dupatta pinned to a loose braid or a low bun stays put far better than one pinned to open hair. Bring the dupatta to the salon if you can, so the stylist can set the style around it.

Questions people ask

What is the best hairstyle for a mehndi night?

A loose braid, a low bun, or a half-up style works best, because it stays neat through henna, dancing, and photographs without feeling stiff.

Should I wash my hair before the mehndi night?

Wash your hair the night before, not on the evening of the function. Slightly unwashed hair holds a braid or a bun better than freshly washed hair.

Can I wear my hair open for a mehndi night?

Yes, if the waves are set well and pinned loosely at the sides. Open hair needs more care during dancing, so a braid or a bun is the safer choice for a long evening.
