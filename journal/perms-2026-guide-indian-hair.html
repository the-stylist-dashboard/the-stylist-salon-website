Perms Are Back: A 2026 Guide for Indian Hair

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 7 min read

Your feed is full of soft, bouncy waves that look effortless, and you know yours are not. You have tried curling irons, overnight braids, and every heatless curl hack. They all fall flat by lunch. A perm promises curls that survive the day. The 2026 version is nothing like the tight frizz-ball perms of the eighties, but it is still a serious chemical commitment. Here is the full picture.

01 The perm is back, but it grew up

The modern perm is about loose waves, beachy texture, and soft volume, not tight ringlets. Digital perms, which use heated rods, give defined, bouncy curls that look styled even when you do nothing. Cold perms give a more natural, tousled wave. Both are a world away from the crunchy perms your mother remembers.

The appeal is obvious: wake up with texture, skip the daily curling iron, and have hair that holds a style through Gurugram's humidity. But a perm is chemistry plus heat, and your hair needs to be strong enough to take it.

02 What Indian hair needs to know

Dense Indian hair holds a perm well, since the curls have plenty to grip. That is the good news. The catch: if your hair is already damaged from bleach, frequent heat, or previous chemical treatments, adding a perm on top is asking for breakage. Perms need a minimum length too. Very short hair cannot wrap around the rods properly, so shoulder length or longer works best.

Colour and perm in the same month is a bad idea. Space them out by several weeks, always with a strand test in between. If your hair has been bleached, be honest about it at the consultation. A good stylist may refuse to perm severely damaged hair, and that refusal is a sign of a good stylist.

03 Digital perm vs cold perm

A digital perm uses heated rods and creates curls that are defined when dry and looser when wet. It suits people who want a polished, bouncy look and are willing to style a little. A cold perm uses no heat during processing and gives softer, more natural-looking waves that look best with a bit of air-drying.

Digital perms generally cost more and take longer, around three to four hours. Cold perms are quicker and gentler. Neither is objectively better. It depends on the curl pattern you want and how much daily styling you will actually do. Bring reference photos to your consultation. Words like beachy mean different things to different people.

04 Aftercare: the curls are fragile at first

For the first 48 hours, do not wash, do not tie your hair up, and try not to get it wet. The curl pattern is still setting. After that, switch to products made for curly hair: a sulphate-free shampoo, a rich conditioner, and a leave-in or curl cream on damp hair.

Ditch the regular towel, since it roughs up the curl. Use a microfiber towel or an old cotton t-shirt to squeeze out water. A diffuser attachment on your dryer keeps the shape, while direct high heat flattens it. Sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase to cut morning frizz. Trim split ends on schedule, because permed ends dry out faster than virgin hair.

05 Who should skip the perm

Skip it if your hair is bleached or severely damaged, if it is very fine and goes limp easily, or if you hate styling your hair. This last one matters more than people think. A perm gives you the curl pattern, but permed hair still needs curl cream, diffusing, and refreshing to look like those photos. If you will not do that, you will end up with undefined frizz instead of waves.

Also skip it right before a big event. Get the perm three to four weeks before a wedding or function, so you have time to learn your hair and fix anything you do not like. Try before you commit: style your hair in waves with a curling iron for a full week and live with it. If you love the look and the maintenance feels fine, a perm will make your life easier. If you are bored by day three, skip the perm.

Questions we hear at the salon

How long does a perm last?

A perm typically lasts 3 to 6 months, loosening gradually as your hair grows. How long the curls hold depends on your hair texture, the perm type, and aftercare. Digital perms often keep their shape a little longer than cold perms. Regular trims and curl-friendly products help the pattern last.

Will a perm damage my hair?

A perm is a chemical treatment, so some dryness is normal, but healthy hair handles it well when done properly. The risk rises sharply on bleached, over-processed, or very fine hair. A strand test before the full treatment is the best predictor of how your hair will respond.

Can I straighten permed hair sometimes?

Yes, you can flat iron permed hair for a straighter look, but frequent heat styling loosens the curl pattern faster and adds damage. Use a heat protectant every time. If you find yourself straightening more days than not, the perm may not have been the right choice for your lifestyle.

How much does a perm cost?

Pricing varies with hair length, density, and whether you choose a digital or cold perm. Digital perms cost more because of the equipment and time involved. Ask for a quote at your consultation, since your stylist needs to see your hair's length and condition to price it accurately.
