01 What party mehndi covers

Party mehndi usually covers the palms and the backs of the hands, with a design that runs up the wrist, and sometimes the feet for a bigger function. The artist asks about your outfit first, because the design should sit well with the colours and the style you will wear. A simple Arabic design suits a modern outfit, while a fuller traditional design suits a heavier, classic look.

Tell the artist how much time you have, because a detailed design takes longer, and a rushed design never looks as clean as one done at a calm pace. If you have a photo of a design you like, bring it, because it saves time and avoids confusion.

02 Designs that suit a party

For a party, a neat Arabic design with bold lines and open spaces looks fresh and modern, and it dries faster than a dense traditional design. A mandala on the palm with a simple border is a safe pick, because it looks good on its own and photographs well. If you want something lighter, a bracelet-style design around the wrist with small motifs on the fingers is pretty without being heavy.

Keep the design simple if your hands will be busy at the party, because a dense design needs time to dry and can smudge if you touch things too soon. A lighter design also suits a short function, where you do not want to spend the whole evening guarding wet mehndi. Ask the artist what will dry in the time you have, and pick the design to fit the day, not the other way round.

03 When to book party mehndi

Book party mehndi a day before the function, so the colour has time to deepen overnight and the hands look their best on the day. Mehndi colour is lightest right after it is applied, and it darkens over the next day, so a same-day sitting can look pale in photos. If you can only come on the day itself, book the earliest slot, so the mehndi has the most time to set before the function starts.

In wedding season, mehndi slots fill fast in Ggn, so book a few days ahead, especially for a weekend function. Tell the salon at booking how many people want mehndi, because a group booking needs more time and more than one artist. A group that books together also gets the designs done in one visit, without a long wait between people.

04 Caring for mehndi after it is applied

Keep the mehndi on for at least four to six hours, longer if you can, because the longer it stays, the deeper the colour. Avoid washing the hands with soap right after the mehndi is removed, because soap fades the colour fast, so use a little oil instead and let the colour settle. Keep the hands away from water for the first few hours, so the stain has time to deepen.
