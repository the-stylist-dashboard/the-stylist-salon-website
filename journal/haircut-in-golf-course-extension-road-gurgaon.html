01 The consultation is the haircut

Most bad haircuts are not a skill problem. They are a briefing problem. The stylist imagined one thing and the client imagined another, and nobody checked before the first cut. A proper consultation covers face shape, hair type, how much time you spend styling each morning, and what you hated about your last cut.

Bring reference photos, but bring two kinds: looks you like and looks you do not want. The second set is often more useful. A photo of a cut on straight, fine hair will not behave the same on thick, wavy hair, and your stylist should tell you that plainly before starting.

If you are growing your hair out, tell your stylist that too, because a growing-out cut is planned differently from a cut meant to hold a shape. The stylist can shape the ends and layers so the awkward middle stage looks intentional rather than neglected. Growing out does not mean skipping trims. Small trims keep the ends healthy, so you keep the length you grew instead of losing it to split ends later.

02 Say the practical things out loud

Tell your stylist about recent colour, bleach, keratin, or smoothening work, because treated hair cuts and falls differently. Say how short you are willing to go in centimetres, not just short or a little off. Say whether you tie your hair up for work or wear it open, because a cut has to work both ways.

If you use heat tools daily, say so. If you never blow dry, say that too. A cut that needs a blow dry every morning is a bad cut for someone who air dries, no matter how good it looks in the salon chair.

03 How often to come back

Most short cuts need a trim every four to six weeks to hold shape. Longer cuts can go six to eight weeks, but split ends should be trimmed before they travel up the hair. Waiting until the ends feel rough usually means losing more length at the next visit than you wanted.

Book the next trim before you leave if your cut has a shape you want to keep. It is easier to hold a shape with small, regular trims than to rebuild it after three months of growth.
