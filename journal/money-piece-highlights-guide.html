The Money Piece: Face-Framing Highlights Explained

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

You want a change, but you are not ready for a full head of highlights. The money piece is the salon's answer to exactly that hesitation: a few bright sections framing the face, done in under an hour, that make the whole head of hair look lighter than it is.

01 What it actually is

A money piece is a set of lightened sections placed along the front hairline, framing the face on both sides. The name comes from the idea that these are the pieces people actually notice, the ones worth the money. Everything behind them can stay dark, and the face still gets all the brightness.

It is the smallest commitment in the highlight family. A full balayage is a half-day appointment. A money piece is usually done inside an hour, and because only the front sections are processed, most of your hair never meets lightener at all.

02 Who it suits

First-time colour clients love it because the risk is contained. Dark-haired clients love it because it delivers visible change without a full-head process. It also works beautifully as an add-on: a money piece in front of existing balayage or global colour refreshes the whole look for a fraction of a full appointment.

One practical consideration: placement follows your parting and your usual hairstyle. If you always wear a centre part, the pieces sit symmetrically. Side partings shift the balance. Mention how you actually wear your hair, not how the reference photo wears hers.

03 The three decisions in the chair

How bright. On dark Indian hair, a money piece typically lands in caramel or honey rather than true blonde. Softer brightness looks natural and grows out gracefully. Bold, high-contrast brightness makes a statement and needs more upkeep.

How wide. Thin ribbons read subtle and expensive. Chunky sections read bold and nineties. There is no wrong answer, but the width should match the energy of the rest of your hair. Fine hair with a thick money piece looks unbalanced.

What tone. Warm caramel and honey flatter warm Indian skin tones. Icy or ashy tones can look striking but also harsh, and they fade into brassiness faster on dark bases. If you are unsure, our balayage vs highlights guide covers how tones behave on Indian skin.

04 Upkeep, honestly

This is the cheapest highlight to maintain. The placement grows out softly, most people refresh every eight to twelve weeks, and a toner in between keeps brassiness away. Because it is only a few sections, the refresh appointment is short.

One caveat: if your base is coloured rather than virgin, the money piece regrowth will show a line where your root colour meets the lightened section. On virgin hair the grow-out is nearly seamless. Your colourist should flag which situation you are in before starting.

05 Mistakes to avoid

Too chunky and it reads as stripes. Too light against a dark base and it looks disconnected instead of blended. And the home-foil money piece, attempted with box bleach in a bathroom mirror, is a regular visitor to our correction chair. Front sections are the most visible hair on your head; they are the worst place to experiment.

Bring your real hair: show up with your hair styled the way you normally wear it. Placement decisions made on a fresh blowout do not always survive your everyday ponytail.

Questions we hear at the salon

What is a money piece in hair colour?

A money piece is a set of lightened sections framing the face along the front hairline. It brightens the face and makes the whole head look lighter with minimal colour. The appointment usually takes under an hour, making it the quickest highlight service on the menu.

Does a money piece suit Indian skin tones?

Yes, when the tone is chosen well. Warm caramel and honey money pieces flatter warm Indian skin tones beautifully. The shades to be careful with are icy or ashy blondes, which can look harsh against warm skin. Your colourist should match the tone to your undertone, not just to the trend.

How long does a money piece last?

The placement grows out softly, so most people refresh it every eight to twelve weeks. A toner refresh in between keeps the tone from going brassy. Because it is only a few sections, the upkeep appointment is short and costs less than a full highlight service.

Can I get a money piece on dark virgin hair?

Absolutely, and it is one of the best first colours for dark virgin hair. The lightening is limited to the front sections, so most of your hair stays untouched. On very dark hair the money piece usually lands in caramel or honey territory rather than blonde, which tends to look more natural anyway.
