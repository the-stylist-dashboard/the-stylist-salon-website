Gel nails are the reason most of our nail clients stopped doing regular polish. The colour goes on smooth, cures hard under a lamp in minutes, and survives two to three weeks of typing, cooking, gym, and daily life without chipping. If you want nails that look done on day fourteen the way they looked on day one, this is the service.

01 Why gel nails win for busy hands

Regular polish chips because it dries by evaporation, which leaves it soft. Gel polish cures under UV or LED light into a hard, flexible layer bonded to the nail. That is the whole secret. Two to three weeks of glossy, chip-free colour is normal.

It suits hands that take a beating: office work, cooking, kids, workouts. If you have ever repainted chips the night before an event, gel ends that cycle. The appointment takes about an hour, and you walk out with nails fully dry. No smudging on the car keys.

02 The prep matters more than the polish

A gel manicure lives or dies on prep. We push back cuticles, gently buff the nail, dehydrate it, and apply a base coat before colour. Skipping steps is how lifting starts. Good prep takes fifteen minutes and buys an extra week of wear.

Hygiene is non-negotiable in nail work. Tools are sterilised between clients, files are single-use, and the workspace is wiped down. If a salon reuses files, walk out. Your nails sit on that equipment for an hour; it should be spotless.

03 Choosing shape, length, and colour

Shape follows your fingers and your life. Almond and oval flatter most hands and snag less than square. If you type all day, keep the length modest. Short gel nails look neat, last longer, and break less.

Nude and sheer shades grow out invisibly, so the manicure looks tidy even in week three. Dark colours show growth sooner but look striking. Tell us what your weeks look like and we will steer you to a shade that still looks good grown out.

04 Gel nails vs extensions

Gel polish sits on your natural nail and adds colour and strength. Extensions add length with gel or acrylic tips, then colour on top. If your nails have decent length, gel polish is enough and kinder. If you want length you do not have, extensions are the honest answer.

Extensions need infills every two to three weeks as the nail grows. Gel polish you just remove and redo. First-timers usually do best starting with gel on natural nails: cheaper, faster, and you learn how your nails behave under gel.

05 Safe removal and nail health

Never peel gel off. Peeling takes the top layer of your natural nail with it, and that damage takes months to grow out. Proper removal is a soak-off: we file the top coat, wrap nails in remover-soaked cotton, wait, and gently push the gel away. Ten to fifteen minutes, no pain.

After removal, nails appreciate cuticle oil and a breather. Most clients go straight into a fresh set, which is fine if removal was done properly. Questions we hear at the salon

How long do gel nails last?

Two to three weeks of chip-free wear is normal with good prep. Nude shades look tidy even longer since growth shows less. Avoid using nails as tools and wear gloves for harsh cleaning.

Do gel nails damage natural nails?

Not when applied and removed properly. Damage comes from peeling gel off or aggressive filing, not the gel itself. Our soak-off removal takes ten to fifteen minutes and leaves the nail intact.

Gel polish or nail extensions: which should I choose?

If your natural nails have decent length, gel polish is enough and kinder. Choose extensions only for length you do not have. First-timers usually do best with gel on natural nails.
