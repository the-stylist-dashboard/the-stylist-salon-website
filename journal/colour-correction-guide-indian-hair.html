Colour Correction: Fixing a Dye Job Gone Wrong

The Stylist Unisex Salon · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

The box promised honey brown. The mirror shows traffic-cone orange. Or the salon went too dark and now your hair looks like a helmet. Colour correction is the unglamorous, multi-hour service that fixes these situations, and it starts with one rule: stop touching it yourself.

01 The usual disasters

Too dark. The most common complaint. Box dye and even salon colour often process darker than expected on porous hair, and "just one shade" can read as three. Orange or brassy. Home lightening on dark Indian hair stalls at the warm stage, leaving a copper that was never in the plan. Patchy. Uneven application, missed sections at the back, roots that took differently from lengths. Banding. Stripes of different shades where colour was overlapped at successive touch-ups.

Every one of these is fixable. None of them is fixed by panic.

02 Why home fixes make it worse

Colour on top of colour goes darker, not better. That is the single most expensive misunderstanding in home colouring. The "just dye it black and start over" instinct buries the problem under a shade that is then extremely hard to lift later. Bleach over orange without a plan creates breakage without fixing the tone. Each layer you add limits what a colourist can do next and raises the price of the eventual fix.

The hardest corrections we see are not single mistakes. They are three stacked attempts, each one trying to fix the last. If your colour has gone wrong, the cheapest thing you can do is nothing. Put the box down and book a consultation.

03 What correction actually involves

A proper correction starts with a consultation and a strand test, not with mixing. The colourist needs your full history: what was used, when, how many times, and whether henna was ever involved. Then the plan is built in stages. Existing unwanted colour is shifted or removed first, and only then is the target shade rebuilt.

Hair health is assessed before anything else. If the hair cannot take another chemical process safely, the honest answer is to wait, trim, condition, and come back. A colourist who refuses to correct damaged hair on the spot is protecting you, not rejecting you. Complex cases are split across two or three appointments spaced weeks apart so the hair recovers between sessions.

04 The cost and time, honestly

Correction costs more than the original service. It involves extra steps, more product and significantly more chair time, and pricing varies with hair length, density and the number of sessions needed. A straightforward fix runs three to five hours. A layered disaster can take multiple visits.

Sometimes the fix is not what you hoped for. Going temporarily darker to even everything out, then lightening gradually over months, is a common and sensible route. If your dream shade needs three sittings, a good colourist says so upfront instead of promising it in one. Our colour fade guide will matter after the correction, because corrected hair needs gentler care than virgin hair.

05 Preventing the next one

Always do a strand test before a dramatic change, even at a salon you trust. Bring realistic reference photos, not filtered ones, and listen when the colourist says your target needs two sittings. Disclose your full history, including the box dye from two years ago that you assume has grown out. It has not, not fully.

Bring the box: if the damage came from box dye, bring the box or a clear photo of it. The formula history changes the correction plan, and guessing wastes everyone's time.

Questions we hear at the salon

What is colour correction in a salon?

Colour correction is the process of fixing unwanted colour results: too dark, too orange, patchy, or banded hair. It usually involves removing or shifting the existing colour and then rebuilding the target shade. It takes longer than a standard colour service and sometimes needs more than one sitting.

Can a bad box dye job be fixed at home?

Rarely, and attempts usually make it worse. Adding more colour on top of a bad result generally goes darker and patchier, not better. The safest move is to stop, avoid further chemical processes, and book a consultation. A colourist needs to see the unaltered damage to plan the fix.

How much does colour correction cost?

Correction costs more than the original colour service because it involves extra steps, more product and more chair time. Pricing varies with hair length, density and how many sessions the fix needs. Expect a consultation first, where the colourist gives you a realistic quote before anything is mixed.

How long does colour correction take?

A straightforward correction takes three to five hours in one sitting. Complex cases, like banded box dye over henna, may need two or three appointments spaced weeks apart so the hair can recover between sessions. Rushing a correction is how hair gets damaged, so patience is part of the service.
